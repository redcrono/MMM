## 1. How the "Corporate Value-Up" Reform Turned KOSPI Giants into Quarterly Payers

Historically, South Korean corporations paid dividends only once a year (with a December 31 record date and an April payout), contributing to the notorious "Korea Discount." Driven by the Financial Services Commission's **Corporate Value-Up Program (기업 밸류업 프로그램)** and aggressive share buyback-and-cancellation mandates, Korea's largest blue-chip companies now distribute steady **quarterly cash dividends (분기배당)** four times a year.

> **`Net Quarterly Cash Dividend = ( Shares Held on Record Date × DPS ) × (1 - 0.154  Withholding Tax)`**

Even more importantly, dozens of KOSPI leaders—including the Big Four banking groups and Hyundai Motor—reformed their corporate charters to adopt **"Dividend Amount First, Record Date Later" (선배당 후투자)**. Instead of blindly locking in shareholders on December 31 before anyone knows the dividend amount, boards now announce the exact KRW dividend per share first and designate a record date in February or March, removing dividend uncertainty!

## 3. Three Pro-Level Korean Dividend Strategies: Preferred Shares, ISA & Capital Reserve Reductions

1. **Buy Korean Preferred Shares (우선주) for Instant Yield Expansion:**
   Unlike US preferred stocks (which behave like fixed-rate bonds), Korean preferred shares (`삼성전자우 005935`, `현대차2우B 005387`) participate 100% in common-stock dividend growth while trading at a **15% to 30% discount** simply because they lack voting rights. Because they pay the exact same (or +₩1–₩100 higher) cash dividend per share as common stock, buying the preferred ticker immediately boosts your annual dividend yield by **0.7%p to 1.8%p**.
2. **Use a Brokerage-Type ISA (중개형 ISA) as Your Dividend Vault:**
   Unlike Pension Savings or IRP accounts (which prohibit buying individual stocks like Samsung Electronics or KB Financial), a **Brokerage-Type ISA** allows you to buy individual Korean stocks alongside ETFs! Inside an ISA, your first **₩2,000,000 (₩4,000,000 for Low-Income/Youth tier)** of net dividends is **100% tax-free**, and any excess is taxed at just **9.9% separate taxation**—completely shielding you from the 15.4% withholding tax and the ₩20M Comprehensive Income Tax cliff.
3. **Target "Capital Reserve Reduction" Tax-Free Dividends (감액배당):**
   When a Korean company converts capital reserves (자본준비금) into retained earnings to fund dividends (as Woori Financial Group and Meritz Financial Group have executed), the National Tax Service classifies the payout as a **return of capital**—meaning retail investors pay **0% dividend tax** even inside a regular taxable brokerage account!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. If I sell my shares on the Ex-Dividend Date (배당락일), do I still receive the dividend?
**Yes, 100%!** As long as you held the shares through the market close (15:30 KST) on the day **before** the Ex-Dividend Date (which is **T-2 business days** before the Record Date), your name is locked onto the Korea Securities Depository shareholder registry for that quarter. You can sell 100% of your shares at 09:00 AM on the Ex-Dividend Date and still receive the full cash dividend next month.

### Q2. Why can't I buy Samsung Electronics or KB Financial inside my Pension Savings (연금저축) or IRP account?
Under the Employee Retirement Benefit Security Act and Income Tax Act, Korean **Pension Savings and IRP accounts** are restricted from holding individual corporate stocks to protect retirement principal—they can only hold **ETFs, mutual funds, REITs, and deposits**. However, you can buy ETFs heavily concentrated in these exact stocks (such as `TIGER Bank High Dividend Plus TOP10` or `KODEX Korea Value-Up`) inside your pension account, or buy the individual stocks directly inside a **Brokerage-Type ISA (중개형 ISA)**.

### Q3. What withholding tax rate applies to foreign non-resident investors vs. foreign tax residents in Korea?
If you live and work in South Korea as a **Tax Resident (거주자)** with an Alien Registration Card, your domestic stock dividends are taxed at the standard domestic rate of **15.4%** (and you can open a tax-free Brokerage ISA). For non-residents living abroad, statutory withholding is 22% reduced to **10%–15%** under most bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Treaties (such as the US–Korea Tax Treaty).
