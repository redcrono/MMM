For high-net-worth individuals, retirees, and long-term savers in South Korea, the biggest obstacle to safe fixed-income investing is the **20,000,000 KRW Comprehensive Financial Income Tax cliff (`금융소득종합과세`)**. When a lump-sum deposit or bond matures and pays out more than 20 million KRW in interest in a single calendar year, the excess is combined with your other income and taxed at progressive rates up to **49.5%**—while simultaneously disqualifying retirees from National Health Insurance Service (`국민건강보험 피부양자`) dependent status.

To solve this problem and encourage household savings in sovereign debt, the Korean government issues **Individual Investor Government Bonds (`개인투자용 국채`)**. With the addition of the **5-year maturity (`5년물`)** alongside the **10-year (`10년물`)** and **20-year (`20년물`)** bonds, investors can now lock in sovereign compound yields without tying up capital for a full decade.

In this 2026 master guide, we break down **the compound interest formula, the 200 million KRW 15.4% separate taxation shield, comparisons with bank fixed deposits and standard KTB bonds, early redemption penalties, and the monthly subscription process**.

## 1. What Are Individual Investor Government Bonds?

Issued directly by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), Individual Investor Government Bonds are savings-type sovereign bonds restricted exclusively to **individual investors** (corporations cannot buy them). Unlike standard Korea Treasury Bonds (`국고채`) traded daily on the bond market, these bonds **cannot be traded on the secondary market or transferred to third parties** (except via inheritance, bequest, or compulsory execution).

In exchange for holding the bond until maturity, the government rewards investors with three powerful advantages:
1. **Bonus Interest Rate (`가산금리`):** Published monthly by MOEF on top of the previous month's sovereign winning auction yield (`표면금리`).
2. **Annual Compound Interest (`연복리`):** Interest is reinvested annually and paid in a lump sum at maturity.
3. **15.4% Separate Taxation (`분리과세`):** Interest on up to 200 million KRW of principal is taxed at a flat 15.4% and excluded from comprehensive income tax.

> **`[Maturity Payout & After-Tax Yield Formula]`**
> * **Total Applied Rate:** `Base Coupon Rate (표면금리) + Monthly Bonus Rate (가산금리)`
> * **Pre-Tax Maturity Payout:** `Principal × (1 + Base Rate + Bonus Rate)^Years` *(Compounded annually)*
> * **After-Tax Net Interest (Within 200M KRW Principal Cap):** `Total Maturity Interest × (1 - 0.154)` *(Flat 15.4% final withholding tax)*

## 2. Why Lump-Sum Maturity Interest Does NOT Trigger Tax or Health Insurance Bombs

Normally, receiving 5, 10, or 20 years of accumulated compound interest in a single calendar year would push your annual financial income far above the **20,000,000 KRW threshold**, triggering both progressive income taxes (up to 49.5%) and immediate loss of NHIS Dependent (`건강보험 피부양자`) status.

However, under **Article 91-23 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act (`조세특례제한법 제91조의23`)**:
- All maturity interest generated from up to **200,000,000 KRW of cumulative purchase principal per person** is subject to **unconditional 14% separate income tax (15.4% including 1.4% local income tax)**.
- Because it is unconditionally separately taxed under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act rather than the general Income Tax Act, **it is excluded from both Comprehensive Financial Income Taxation AND National Health Insurance (NHIS) income assessments**.

## 4. Three Critical Rules Before Subscribing (Early Redemption Penalties)

1. **1-Year Lock-Up Period:** You cannot redeem the bond during the first 12 months after issuance. Early redemption (`중도환매`) is only available starting from the **13th month** after purchase, within the government's monthly redemption quota.
2. **Loss of All 3 Benefits Upon Early Redemption:** If you redeem before maturity, **you forfeit 100% of the bonus rate (`가산금리`), annual compounding (`연복리`), and 15.4% separate taxation (`분리과세`)**. You will receive only simple interest at the base coupon rate, and that interest will be subject to standard comprehensive income tax rules.
3. **No Collateralized Loans Allowed:** Unlike bank time deposits, Individual Investor Government Bonds **cannot be pledged as collateral for loans (`담보대출·질권 설정 금지`)**. Only invest capital that you will not need before the 5-, 10-, or 20-year maturity date.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Is the 200,000,000 KRW limit an annual limit or a cumulative principal limit?
Both exist, so distinguish them carefully:
- **Annual Purchase Limit:** You can subscribe up to **200,000,000 KRW per calendar year**.
- **15.4% Separate Taxation Principal Limit:** The separate tax shield applies to a **cumulative held principal of up to 200,000,000 KRW per person**. Once a 5-year bond matures and returns its principal, that principal quota becomes available again for new separate-tax bond purchases.

### Q2. What happens if the bondholder passes away before maturity?
Although third-party transfers are prohibited, **transfer by inheritance (`상속`) is legally permitted**. Heirs can open a dedicated account, receive the inherited bonds intact, and hold them until the original maturity date to preserve the bonus rate and compound interest (or request early redemption due to inheritance).

### Q3. Should I buy in a Dedicated Account or an IRP/DC Retirement Pension Account?
- Use the **Mirae Asset Dedicated Account** if you want the **5-year bond** or if you are investing large lump sums (up to 200M KRW) outside your pension to avoid the 49.5% Comprehensive Financial Income Tax.
- Use a **DC/IRP Retirement Pension Account** (available for 10yr/20yr bonds) if you want to claim the **year-end pension tax credit (up to 9M KRW/yr at 13.2%～16.5%)** and defer all interest tax until age 55+, when withdrawals are taxed at the low **3.3%～5.5% pension income tax rate**.
