In South Korea, over 75% of household wealth among retirees aged 60 and older is locked inside residential real estate. Many retirees live in apartments worth 600 million to 1.5 billion KRW yet struggle with monthly cash flow—or worry that earning extra pension or dividend income above 20 million KRW per year will disqualify them as dependents (`피부양자`) under the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS).

The **Korea Housing Finance Corporation (HF) Housing Pension (`주택연금`)** solves both problems simultaneously. Backed by a sovereign guarantee from the Korean government, it allows homeowners aged 55+ to convert their home equity into lifetime monthly cash payouts without selling their home, while keeping 100% of their NHIS dependent status intact and slashing annual property taxes by 25%.

In this 2026 master guide, we cover **eligibility rules under the 1.2 billion KRW assessed value cap, estimated monthly payouts by age and home price, Preferential Housing Pension benefits, property tax and NHIS advantages, and the Trust vs. Mortgage registration methods**.

## 1. 2026 Eligibility Requirements for the HF Housing Pension

### ① Age & Nationality Requirement: Age 55+
- Either the homeowner or their legal spouse must be **at least 55 years old** (`만 55세 이상`).
- Because payouts continue until both spouses pass away, the monthly payout calculation is based on the **age of the younger spouse**.

### ② Property Value Cap: Assessed Value (`공시가격`) <= 1.2 Billion KRW
- Your home's **Official Assessed Value (`공시가격`)** must be **1.2 billion KRW or less** at the time of enrollment. Since official assessed values in Korea typically represent 65%～70% of market prices, apartments with a **real market value (`시세`) of roughly 1.6 billion to 1.8 billion KRW** qualify.
- Eligible property types include apartments, detached houses, multi-family units, residential officetels (`주거용 오피스텔`), and senior welfare housing (`노인복지주택`).

### ③ Multi-Homeowner Rules
- Even if a couple owns **2 or more homes**, they can enroll immediately without selling if the **combined Official Assessed Value of all owned homes is 1.2 billion KRW or less**.
- If a 2-homeowner couple's combined assessed value exceeds 1.2 billion KRW, they can still enroll immediately on the condition that they **sell the non-occupied home within 3 years**.

### ④ 2026 Residency Exceptions (Hospitalization & Silver Towns)
- Normally, the homeowner or spouse must reside in the collateralized property.
- However, under expanded 2026 rules, if a 1-homeowner couple moves into a **nursing hospital, long-term care facility, or senior welfare facility (Silver Town)**, they remain eligible for the Housing Pension AND are allowed to **rent out their original home on a monthly rent (`월세`) basis** to generate dual retirement income.

## 3. Three Huge Tax & Health Insurance Advantages

### ① 0% Impact on National Health Insurance (`건강보험료 피부양자`)
In Korea, if a retiree's public pension (`국민연금` / `공무원연금`) or financial income exceeds **20,000,000 KRW per year** (or 10,000,000 KRW if property tax base exceeds 540M KRW), they lose their NHIS Dependent status and face hundreds of thousands of won in monthly regional health insurance premiums.
- **Housing Pension payouts are legally loan proceeds, NOT income.**
- Even if you receive **3,000,000 KRW every month (36,000,000 KRW/year)** from the Housing Pension, it adds **0 KRW to your taxable income** and **never triggers NHIS dependent disqualification**.

### ② 25% Annual Property Tax Reduction (`재산세 25% 감면`)
Single-homeowner households enrolled in the Housing Pension receive an automatic **25% reduction on their annual July/September Property Tax (`재산세 본세`)** for the portion of assessed value up to 500,000,000 KRW, plus exemptions on mortgage registration license taxes (`등록면허세`).

### ③ Up to 2,000,000 KRW Annual Income Tax Deduction on Accrued Interest
If you have other pension or comprehensive income subject to income tax, the accrued interest on your Housing Pension balance qualifies for an income deduction of up to **2,000,000 KRW per year**.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. What happens if I move to a different house or my apartment undergoes reconstruction (`재건축`)?
**You do not need to cancel your Housing Pension.** You can transfer the collateral to your new home (`담보주택 변경`) and continue receiving payouts (adjusted for any price difference), or participate in apartment reconstruction while keeping your Housing Pension active.

### Q2. Can I cancel the Housing Pension if my home price surges in the future?
**Yes.** You can repay the accumulated payout principal, interest, and guarantee fees at any time to terminate the contract with **zero early repayment penalty (`중도상환수수료 면제`)**, though re-enrollment on the same property is restricted for 3 years after cancellation.

### Q3. Can I enroll if I still have a tenant's Jeonse deposit or a bank mortgage on the home?
You cannot have a third-party lease deposit (`전세보증금`) holding senior lien priority unless you use the Housing Pension's **Lump-Sum Withdrawal (`일시인출금`)** to pay off the existing mortgage or return the tenant's deposit upon enrollment.
