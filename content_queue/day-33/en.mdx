## 1. Why Trillions of Won Are Moving from Korean Banks to Brokerages via `실물이전`

Prior to the launch and 2026 expansion of South Korea's **Retirement Pension In-Kind Transfer System (`퇴직연금 실물이전 제도`)**, transferring an Individual Retirement Pension (**IRP**) or Defined Contribution (**DC**) account from a commercial bank (KB, Shinhan, Hana, Woori) to a securities brokerage required **100% forced liquidation (`전액 현금화 매도`)**. Investors had to break maturing high-interest time deposits—forfeiting their contracted interest rate—and sell all funds to move cash across institutions.

Today, the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and Ministry of Employment and Labor mandate **In-Kind Transfers (`실물이전`)** across 37+ banks, brokerages, and insurers, and are actively expanding the system to allow direct **DC-to-External-IRP in-kind rollovers** upon job changes or retirement.

> **`20-Year Compound Fee Savings = KRW 100,000,000 IRP Balance × Bank Annual Fee (0.30%) Saved via Zero-Fee Online Brokerage IRP = Over KRW 8,500,000 in Preserved Retirement Wealth`**

## 3. Bypassing the 30% IRP Safe-Asset Rule with Qualifying TDF 2050/2060 ETFs

Under the Act on the Guarantee of Employees' Retirement Benefits, both **DC** and **IRP** accounts enforce a strict **70% Risky-Asset Ceiling (`위험자산 70% 한도`)**. You can invest at most 70% of your account in pure equity ETFs (such as `TIGER US S&P500` or `KODEX US Nasdaq100`), while the remaining **30% must be held in statutory Safe Assets (`안전자산`)**.

Instead of leaving that 30% in a low-yield 2.8% bank deposit, sophisticated investors use **Qualifying Target Date Funds (`적격 TDF`)**:
- Financial regulators classify FSS-approved **TDFs (including high-vintage `TDF 2050` and `TDF 2060` ETFs)** as **100% Safe Assets**, even though a 2055/2060 vintage TDF invests up to **78%–80% of its portfolio in global equities** and only 20% in bonds!
- By allocating **70% to US S&P500 / Nasdaq100 ETFs** and **30% to a Qualifying TDF 2055/2060 ETF**, your effective portfolio equity exposure reaches **`70% + (30% × 80%) = 94%`**, maximizing long-term tax-deferred compounding without violating the 70% limit.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Does transferring my IRP in-kind trigger the 16.5% early withdrawal tax or reset my original account opening date?
**A.** **Neither!** An institutional pension transfer (`계약이전 / 실물이전`) is a tax-free trustee-to-trustee rollover. You pay **KRW 0 in tax**, and your **original IRP enrollment date (`최초 가입일`) is 100% inherited** by the new brokerage account—preserving your tenure toward the post-age-55 pension withdrawal limits.

### Q2. As a foreign worker leaving my Korean company, why am I forced to open an IRP to receive my Severance Pay (`퇴직금`)?
**A.** Under Korean labor law, employers are legally required to deposit statutory severance pay (`퇴직급여`, for employees aged under 55 where severance exceeds KRW 3,000,000) into a **Mandatory Severance IRP Account (`퇴직금 수령용 IRP`)** rather than a regular checking account. When you close that IRP to take a lump sum upon departing Korea, the bank withholds regular **Retirement Income Tax (`퇴직소득세`, typically 3%–8%)**—NOT the 16.5% penalty tax (which only applies to voluntary tax-credit contributions).

### Q3. Why did my Default Option (`디폴트옵션`) trigger automatically when my time deposit matured?
**A.** If you hold a time deposit inside a DC or IRP account and fail to give new investment instructions within **4 weeks after maturity** (soon to be notified 2 weeks prior to maturity under FSS reforms), your money is automatically swept into the **Pre-Designated Default Option (`사전지정운용제도`)** you selected when opening the account. Always set your Default Option to a medium- or high-performing TDF portfolio rather than an ultra-low-yield principal-guaranteed option.
