## 1. Why the NTS Sends a Tax Bill in November—and Who Is Exempt

Every November, sole proprietors (`개인사업자`), real estate landlords (`부동산임대업자`), and high-earning independent contractors in South Korea receive a **Comprehensive Income Tax Interim Prepayment Notice (`종합소득세 중간예납 고지서`)** from the National Tax Service (NTS). Many foreign entrepreneurs and first-time business owners panic, assuming they are being double-taxed after already paying their annual tax bill in May.

Under **Article 65 of the South Korean Income Tax Act**, the November Interim Prepayment is **not an extra tax**—it is an advance withholding of **half (Jan 1 to June 30) of your current year's estimated income tax**. Every single Won you pay in November is credited 100% as **Prepaid Tax (`기납부세액`)** on your final Comprehensive Income Tax return next May. If your final tax liability next May turns out to be lower than your November prepayment, the NTS refunds the excess in cash.

> **`Standard NTS November Interim Tax Bill = [Prior Tax Year's Final Comprehensive Income Tax Liability - Prior Year's Withheld Tax] × 50%`**

However, you are **completely exempt** from the November interim prepayment if you fall into any of the following three statutory categories:
1. **The KRW 500,000 De Minimis Exemption (`소액부징수`):** If 50% of your prior year's net tax liability is **less than KRW 500,000**, the NTS cancels the November notice automatically.
2. **First-Year New Businesses (`신규 개업자`):** If you registered your Korean business (`사업자등록`) for the first time during the current calendar year, you have no prior-year tax baseline and owe nothing in November.
3. **Wage Earners & Pure 3.3% Withholding Freelancers:** Salaried employees (`근로소득자`) and independent contractors whose sole income already has the 3.3% business withholding tax deducted at source generally do not receive a separate November prepayment bill.

## 2. How to Slash Your Bill When Business Drops: Estimated Interim Filing (`추계액 신고`)

Because the standard November bill is mechanically calculated as 50% of *last year's* tax, a serious cash-flow crisis arises if your business boomed last year but suffered a severe downturn or temporary closure during the first half (Jan 1–June 30) of this year.

To protect struggling business owners, Article 65(3) of the Income Tax Act allows you to reject the NTS bill and file an **Estimated Interim Tax Return (`중간예납 추계액 신고`)** whenever your annualized Jan–June estimated tax is **less than 30% of your prior year's tax**.

## 3. Interest-Free Installment Rules (`분할납부`) for Bills Over KRW 10,000,000

If your November interim prepayment bill exceeds **KRW 10,000,000**, Korean tax law grants you an automatic, interest-free right to split the payment across two dates (November 30 and early February of the following year):

> **`Interest-Free Installment Formula:`**
> - **`If Total Interim Tax is KRW 10,000,001 to KRW 20,000,000: Pay exactly KRW 10,000,000 in November, and defer the excess above KRW 10M until February 2.`**
> - **`If Total Interim Tax exceeds KRW 20,000,000: Pay 50% in November, and defer the remaining 50% until February 2.`**

You do not even need to submit a separate approval form to split your payment—simply log in to Hometax (`hometax.go.kr`), generate the **First Installment Payment Slip (`분납세액 제외 고지서`)**, and pay the November portion. The NTS will automatically mail you a second bill in January for the February installment.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Do I need to log into Wetax to pay an extra 10% Local Income Tax (`개인지방소득세`) in November?
**A.** **No!** Unlike the May annual tax return (where you must pay 10% Local Income Tax separately on Wetax), South Korea abolished the November interim prepayment for Local Income Tax. In November, you only pay the **National Tax (`국세`)** on Hometax; your full-year Local Income Tax is settled in a single payment next May.

### Q2. I closed my business (`폐업신고`) earlier this year, so why did I still receive a November tax bill?
**A.** Because the NTS computer system generates November prepayment notices automatically based on **last year's tax return (filed in May)**, business owners who closed shop during the current year still receive the notice. Do **not** ignore the notice and do **not** overpay: simply file the **Estimated Interim Tax Return (`중간예납 추계액 신고`)** on Hometax before November 30 reflecting your actual 2026 pre-closure income (or loss) so the bill is legally reduced or zeroed out.

### Q3. What is the penalty if I miss the November 30 payment deadline?
**A.** Missing the statutory deadline triggers an immediate **3% Late Payment Penalty (`납부지연가산세 3%`)** on the unpaid tax the very next day, plus a daily accruing interest penalty of **0.022% per day (approx. 8.03% per annum)**. If you are short on cash, apply for a formal **Payment Deadline Extension (`납부기한 연장 신청`)** on Hometax at least 3 business days before November 30 to avoid the 3% penalty.
