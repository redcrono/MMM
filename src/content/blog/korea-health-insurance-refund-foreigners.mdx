## 1. Why Foreign Residents Overpay NHIS Premiums in South Korea

Under the National Health Insurance Act of South Korea, all registered foreign nationals (ARC holders) staying in Korea for 6 months or longer are automatically enrolled in the **National Health Insurance Service (NHIS, 국민건강보험)**. While workplace subscribers (직장가입자) split their monthly contribution 50/50 with their employer (approx. 3.545% employee share of monthly salary plus long-term care insurance), local subscribers (지역가입자—including freelancers, language students, F-visa self-employed, and unemployed expats between jobs) are billed a statutory **minimum average premium of roughly KRW 150,000 per month** unless they qualify for specific visa reductions (such as D-2/D-4 student discounts).

Because NHIS billing cycles run on an administrative lag between the Ministry of Justice (Immigration), the National Tax Service, and the NHIS registry, tens of thousands of expats inadvertently overpay premiums every year.

> **`Total Claimable NHIS Refund = [Retroactive Workplace Overlap / Dependent Adjustment Premiums] + [Annual Covered Co-Payments Paid - Income-Tier Statutory Out-of-Pocket Cap (KRW 870k–10.5M)]`**

The three most common triggers for **Premium Overpayment Refunds (과오납 보험료 환급)** among foreign residents are:
1. **Dual Billing During Job Transitions:** When you leave a Korean company on the 10th of the month and start a new contract the following month, NHIS automatically switches you to a Local Subscriber (지역가입자) and bills you ～KRW 150,000. Once your new employer retroactively reports your workplace enrollment—or if you prove dependent eligibility under a working spouse—the local premium paid during that gap becomes 100% refundable.
2. **Retroactive Dependent Registration (피부양자 소급 취득):** If your foreign spouse enters Korea on an F-3 or F-2 visa and receives a separate local subscriber bill before you submit the Family Relationship Certificate to register them as your workplace dependent, registering within the statutory window cancels the local bill and triggers a cash refund of any paid amounts.
3. **April Year-End Health Insurance Settlement (4월 건보료 연말정산 환급):** If your actual taxable gross salary reported in February was lower than the estimated monthly base salary (`보수월액`) your company used during the previous year, NHIS credits or refunds the overpaid difference in your April paycheck.

## 2. Out-of-Pocket Cap Refunds (본인부담상한제) & Refund Types Compared

Even more lucrative than premium adjustments is the **Out-of-Pocket Maximum Cap (본인부담상한제)**. Whenever you undergo surgery, childbirth, hospitalization, or chronic treatment in Korea, you pay 20% (inpatient) to 30–60% (outpatient) of NHIS-covered medical items (`급여 항목`). If your cumulative annual covered co-payments across all hospitals and pharmacies between January 1 and December 31 exceed your income-decile ceiling, NHIS refunds 100% of the excess directly to your Korean bank account around late August of the following year.

## 3. Critical Rules for Expats Leaving Korea or Adding Dependents

### Leaving Korea Permanently: How to Claim Before Closing Your Bank Account
If you are finishing your contract (E-2, E-7, D-10, F-2) and leaving South Korea permanently, **never close your Korean bank account before settling your NHIS status**. If you depart Korea on or before the 1st of the month, you owe zero NHIS premium for that month; if you depart on or after the 2nd, workplace premiums are settled up to your final employment date. If you have an unclaimed refund—or expect an Out-of-Pocket Cap refund in August after you have already left Korea—you can either:
1. Keep one Korean bank account open with online banking active, OR
2. Submit a **Power of Attorney for Refund Receipt (환급금 수령 위임장)** along with a copy of your ARC/Passport at a regional NHIS branch before departure, authorizing a trusted friend or colleague in Korea to receive the deposit into their domestic account.

### The 2024–2026 Foreign Dependent 6-Month Rule
Under the revised National Health Insurance Act, a foreign worker's **legal spouse and minor children under 19** can still be registered as NHIS workplace dependents (`피부양자`) immediately upon ARC registration without waiting 6 months. However, **foreign parents, parents-in-law, or adult siblings** moving to Korea must reside in Korea for at least **6 consecutive months** (or hold specific long-term F-2/F-5/F-6 residency statuses) before they can be added as non-paying dependents.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. I received a text message or email about an NHIS refund with a web link. Is it real or a smishing scam?
**A.** Be extremely cautious: **NHIS never sends clickable URL links inside SMS messages asking you to enter your bank account PIN or security card numbers.** Official NHIS refund notifications are sent via **postal mail** to your registered ARC address or via verified **KakaoTalk Public Notification (국민비서 / 공단 알림톡)**. Always ignore SMS links and check your balance directly inside the official `The건강보험` mobile app or by calling `033-811-2000`.

### Q2. Does an Out-of-Pocket Cap refund (본인부담상한제) cover non-insured hospital items (비급여)?
**A.** No. The statutory Out-of-Pocket Cap applies strictly to **NHIS-covered medical services (`급여 본인부담금`)**—such as standard ward beds, covered MRI/CT scans, surgeries, and prescription drugs. Non-covered items (`비급여`), such as voluntary 1-person VIP hospital rooms, cosmetic procedures, or non-covered manual therapy (`도수치료`), are excluded from the NHIS cap calculation (though they may be claimable through private Korean indemnity insurance, `실손의료보험`).

### Q3. What happens if I leave Korea for a 2-month vacation while enrolled as a Local Subscriber?
**A.** If a foreign Local Subscriber departs South Korea for **more than 30 consecutive days (1 month)**, NHIS temporarily suspends your monthly health insurance billing starting from the month following your departure date until you re-enter Korea (provided your visa/ARC remains valid). If you already pre-paid via auto-debit during the month you were abroad for over 30 days, that payment is recorded as an overpayment and can be refunded upon your return.
