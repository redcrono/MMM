## 1. Why the Yellow Umbrella Fund Is the Ultimate Tax Shield for Entrepreneurs

In South Korea, salaried employees receive statutory severance pay (퇴직금) upon leaving a company. Sole proprietors (개인사업자), independent contractors/freelancers (3.3% withholding taxpayers with continuous business activity), and small corporate CEOs have no such safety net. To bridge this gap, the Korea Federation of SMEs (중소기업중앙회 - KBIZ) operates the **Yellow Umbrella Mutual Aid Fund (노란우산공제)**.

What separates the Yellow Umbrella Fund from ordinary bank savings or private pensions is its **Income Deduction (소득공제)** mechanism. When you file your May Comprehensive Income Tax (종합소득세) return, every Won contributed up to your bracket limit is subtracted directly from your **Taxable Income Base (과세표준)** before your marginal tax rate is applied.

$$	ext{Annual Tax Refund / Savings} = 	ext{Annual Contribution (up to Limit)} 	imes 	ext{Marginal Income Tax Rate (6.6\% to 49.5\%)}$$

Furthermore, your principal grows at a **quarterly variable compound interest rate (연복리, currently ~3.3% baseline)**, meaning you enjoy guaranteed risk-free compounding on top of an immediate 16.5% to 44.0% tax reduction.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Can I combine the Yellow Umbrella Fund with Pension Savings and IRP?
**Yes—and doing so creates a ₩15,000,000 tax shield!** The Yellow Umbrella Fund's ₩2M–₩6M Income Deduction is completely separate from the **₩9,000,000 Pension Savings + IRP Tax Credit** limit. A sole proprietor or freelancer who maxes out both shelters up to ₩15,000,000 of annual income from the National Tax Service.

### Q2. What happens if I cancel the account early without closing my business?
**Beware of the 16.5% Other Income Tax penalty!** The Yellow Umbrella Fund is designed as a retirement/business-closure reserve. If you voluntarily cancel (임의해지) while your business is still active and healthy, the NTS claws back your past tax deductions by levying a **16.5% Other Income Tax (기타소득세)** on your payout. However, if you close your business (폐업), retire at age 60+ after 10 years, or suffer a natural disaster/illness, your payout is taxed under the highly favorable **Retirement Income Tax (퇴직소득세)** regime (typically only 3%–5% effective tax).

### Q3. Can foreign nationals with a Korean Business Registration enroll?
**Yes.** Any foreign resident holding a valid visa permitting business or freelance activity (such as F-2, F-5, F-6, D-8, or E-series with authorization) and a registered Korean Business Registration Certificate or 3.3% business income record is eligible to enroll and claim the exact same income tax deductions.
