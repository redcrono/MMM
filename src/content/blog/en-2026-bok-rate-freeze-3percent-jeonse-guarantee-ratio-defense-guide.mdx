## 1. Why Retail Bank Loan Rates Stay High Despite the BOK 3.00% Rate Freeze

When the Bank of Korea (BOK) Monetary Policy Board announced its decision to hold the benchmark policy rate at **3.00%**, many homeowners and tenants expected commercial bank mortgage (`주택담보대출`) and Jeonse loan (`전세자금대출`) rates to stabilize or decline immediately. Instead, borrowers renewing their leases or applying for new mortgages at KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Hana, and Woori Bank continue to face effective interest rates hovering between **4.2% and 5.2%**.

This disconnect occurs because your retail loan rate is governed by two separate forces: **market funding benchmarks** and **regulatory spread controls**.

> **`Final Retail Loan Interest Rate = Market Benchmark (5-Yr AAA Bank Bond or COFIX) + Bank Credit/Regulatory Spread (가산금리) - Preferential Discounts (우대금리)`**

While the BOK's 3.00% rate pause stabilized the 5-year AAA bank bond yield (`금융채 5년물`), the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) have maintained strict **annual household loan volume caps (`가계부채 총량관리`)** on commercial banks. To ration credit without violating regulatory quotas, banks have artificially widened their internal **Regulatory Spread (`가산금리`)** and trimmed preferential rate discounts (`우대금리`). In short, commercial loan rates reflect a policy surcharge designed to decouple bank credit from Seoul real estate speculation.

## 2. How the Jeonse Loan Guarantee Cut (100% → 80%) Affects Tenants

Historically, South Korea's Jeonse loan market operated on near-total state-backed credit guarantees: **HUG (Housing & Urban Guarantee Corp.)** and **SGI Seoul Guarantee** insured **100%** of a tenant's Jeonse loan principal, while **HF (Korea Housing Finance Corp.)** insured **90%**. Because banks bore almost zero default risk on the borrower, they approved Jeonse loans with minimal scrutiny of the tenant's personal Debt Service Ratio (DSR) or credit score.

Under the FSC's tightened household debt framework, reducing the standard Jeonse guarantee coverage ratio to **80% (or lower for high-value capital-region leases)** fundamentally alters how banks underwrite your lease loan.

## 3. Why 5-Year Periodic Fixed Loans (`주기형`) Beat Variable COFIX Loans Right Now

Borrowers often wonder whether they should select a **6-Month Variable Rate (`변동금리`)** in anticipation of future BOK rate cuts in 2027, or lock in a **5-Year Periodic Fixed Rate (`5년 주기형 고정금리`)**. Under current Korean bond pricing and Stress DSR regulations, the **5-Year Periodic Fixed option wins decisively** for three mathematical reasons:
1. **Immediate Yield Curve Inversion Savings:** Because bond traders price in long-term monetary easing ahead of time, 5-year bank bond yields are currently trading lower than short-term 6-month COFIX funding costs, giving periodic fixed borrowers an instant **0.30%p to 0.50%p lower rate from Day 1**.
2. **Higher Borrowing Limit Under Stress DSR:** Under Stage 2/3 Stress DSR rules, variable-rate loans are penalized with 100% of the stress buffer rate, slashing your maximum loan limit. By contrast, 5-year periodic fixed loans receive a **60%–70% reduction in the stress rate buffer**, unlocking tens of millions of Won in additional borrowing capacity.
3. **Zero Prepayment Penalty After 36 Months:** In South Korea, mandatory mortgage prepayment penalties (`중도상환수수료`, typically 1.2%–1.4%) expire completely after **3 years (36 months)**—even on a 5-year fixed or 30-year mortgage. Thus, locking in a cheaper 5-year periodic rate today gives you lower monthly payments for 3 years plus a free refinancing option at Month 37.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. If I buy an apartment while living in a Jeonse home with a Jeonse loan, will the bank recall my loan immediately?
**A.** It depends on the price and location of the purchased home. If you acquire ownership of an apartment valued at **over KRW 300,000,000 inside a speculative/regulated district (`투기·투기과열지구`)**, your Jeonse loan is subject to mandatory recall (`기한의 이익 상실`), unless the purchased apartment has an existing tenant whose lease term has not yet expired. Even outside regulated zones, becoming a homeowner blocks you from extending HUG/HF policy Jeonse loans at maturity, and acquiring **2 or more homes** triggers an immediate ban on Jeonse loan renewals. However, winning an **apartment pre-sale right (`분양권`)** does not count as home ownership until final building completion and registration (`보존등기`).

### Q2. Why did my COFIX-linked loan rate tick up slightly even though the Bank of Korea froze the base rate at 3.00%?
**A.** **COFIX (Cost of Funds Index)** is calculated on the 15th of every month based on the actual weighted-average interest rates that 8 major Korean commercial banks paid on time deposits (`정기예금`), savings, and bank bonds during the preceding month. When banks launch high-interest 1-year deposit specials in the fourth quarter to retain maturing deposits, COFIX can rise by 0.03%p–0.08%p even when the BOK base rate stays completely flat.

### Q3. My landlord agreed to lower my Jeonse deposit by KRW 50,000,000 (`감액 갱신`). Do I have to pay that KRW 50M back to the bank?
**A.** Only if your existing loan balance exceeds **80% (or 90% for youth policy loans) of the new, lower Jeonse deposit**. For example, if your original Jeonse deposit was KRW 300M with a KRW 200M loan, and your new deposit is lowered to KRW 250M, 80% of KRW 250M is still KRW 200M. In that case, you do **not** have to repay a single Won to the bank—you can keep the entire KRW 50M returned by your landlord as liquid cash!
