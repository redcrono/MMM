Every tax season in South Korea, employees who quit their job mid-year (`중도퇴사자`) or moved to a new company (`이직자`) face two costly mistakes: either they leave hundreds of thousands of won in unclaimed tax refunds sitting with the National Tax Service (NTS), or they accidentally claim credit card and medical expenses incurred while unemployed—triggering an audit penalty.

In this 2026 guide, we explain **how to read Line 74 (`결정세액`) of your withholding receipt, which deductions apply strictly to your employment period vs. the entire calendar year, how job changers combine previous and current salaries without awkward HR calls, and the 4-step May Hometax refund process**.

---

## 1. Two Scenarios: Mid-Year Resignee vs. Mid-Year Job Changer

### Scenario A: Quit Mid-Year and Did Not Re-Employ by December 31 (`중도퇴사자`)
When you resign mid-year, your former employer automatically performs a basic tax settlement on your final paycheck using only the standard personal deduction (`기본공제 150만 원`) and statutory social insurance deductions. Because the NTS Simplification Portal is not open mid-year, **none of your credit cards, medical expenses, monthly rent, or pension contributions were deducted**.
- **What to do:** Skip the January company filing window and file directly on **NTS Hometax between May 1 and May 31** (or file a 5-year Retroactive Refund Claim, `경정청구`, for past years).

### Scenario B: Changed Jobs Within the Same Calendar Year (`이직자`)
If you worked at Company A from January to May and joined Company B in July, Korean tax law requires your **combined annual earned income (Company A + Company B)** to be settled together:
- **Option 1 (Standard):** Download your `근로소득 원천징수영수증` (Earned Income Withholding Receipt) from Company A and submit it to Company B's HR team in January.
- **Option 2 (Privacy / Missed January):** Settle only Company B's salary in January, then log in to **NTS Hometax in May (May 1 ～ May 31)** to combine Company A + Company B yourself with zero penalty!

## 2. Check "Line 74 (`결정세액`)" First Before Filing

Before spending time gathering receipts as a mid-year resignee, open your `근로소득 원천징수영수증` on Hometax and look at **Line 74 (`결정세액`, Determined Tax)**:

> **`[How to Read Lines 74–76 on Your Withholding Tax Receipt]`**
> * **Line 74 (`결정세액`, Determined Tax):** The actual income tax retained by the government after your departure settlement. **This is the maximum additional refund you can recover in May!**
> * **Line 75 (`기납부세액`, Prepaid Tax):** Total tax withheld from your monthly paychecks before quitting.
> * **Line 76 (`차감징수세액`, Net Withheld/Refunded at Departure):** `Line 74 - Line 75`.

- If **Line 74 (`결정세액`) is `0 KRW`**, your employer already refunded 100% of your prepaid taxes on your final paycheck—you have **0 KRW left to recover** and do not need to file in May!
- If **Line 74 (`결정세액`) is greater than `0 KRW`** (e.g., `620,000 KRW`), you can recover up to that exact amount (plus 10% local income tax) by adding your card, medical, rent, and pension deductions in May.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Are Unemployment Benefits (`실업급여 / 구직급여`) or Parental Leave Benefits (`육아휴직급여`) taxed in May?
**No.** Under the Income Tax Act, Unemployment Benefits (`구직급여`) and Parental Leave / Maternity Leave benefits received from Employment Insurance are **100% tax-exempt (`비과세 소득`)** and are never added to your taxable income.

### Q2. What if I forgot to file in May for a job I quit 2 or 3 years ago?
You can still claim 100% of your refund for up to **5 years** after the original filing deadline by submitting an online **Request for Rectification (`경정청구`)** on NTS Hometax (`[종합소득세 신고 -> 근로소득 신고 -> 경정청구]`).

### Q3. What if I failed to combine my previous employer's salary during January Year-End Tax Settlement?
Simply log in to NTS Hometax between **May 1 and May 31** and file a Combined Earned Income Return (`종합소득세 확정신고`). As long as you combine both salaries and pay/settle by May 31, **zero penalty tax is charged**.
