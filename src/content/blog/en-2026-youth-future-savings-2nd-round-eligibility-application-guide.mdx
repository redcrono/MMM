## 1. Two Government-Matched Wealth Engines for Young Adults in Korea

With commercial bank deposit rates hovering near 3.0%–3.3% before tax, young adults aged 19 to 34 in South Korea have access to two extraordinary state-subsidized savings programs that multiply principal through direct government cash grants and tax exemptions:
1. **Youth Jump Account (청년도약계좌 - Operated by KINFA):** Open monthly to working young adults earning up to ₩75,000,000 (household median income ≤ 250%). Depositing up to ₩700,000/month builds a **~₩50,000,000 lump sum** over 5 years (or ~₩27M over 3 years with full tax-free privileges).
2. **Youth Tomorrow Savings Account (청년내일저축계좌 - Operated by MOHW):** Targeted at low-to-moderate income working youth (household median income ≤ 100%). When you save just **₩100,000/month**, the government matches **₩100,000/month (1:1 match)** or **₩300,000/month (1:3 match for median income ≤ 50%)**, turning ₩3.6M of your principal into **₩7.2M to ₩14.4M + bank interest** in just 3 years!

## 3. Passing the Household Income Test: How Resident Registration Affects Approval

The #1 reason high-saving young adults are rejected during the Youth Jump Account screening is **Household Median Income (가구 중위소득 250% 기준)**:
- **Who Counts in Your Household:** The Korea Inclusive Finance Agency (KINFA) includes **only your spouse, parents, and children** listed on your current Resident Registration Card (주민등록등본). Adult siblings (형제·자매) are automatically excluded even if they live at the same address.
- **How to Legally Separate Your Household (세대분리):** If you live independently in a studio or shared house while your address is still registered at your high-earning parents' home, complete your **Move-In Report (전입신고)** to your actual residence *before* applying. Once registered as a 1-person household, only your personal income is evaluated against the 1-person 250% median income ceiling (~₩6,000,000/month), guaranteeing approval!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Can university students with part-time income or military service members apply?
**Yes!** As long as you have **any NTS-reported earned or business income** (even a part-time job reported on a withholding tax receipt or May comprehensive income tax return), you qualify for the highest government matching tier (up to ₩33,000/month). Furthermore, under the revised rules, **military service pay (군 장병 급여)** for enlisted soldiers and **parental leave benefits (육아휴직급여)** are officially recognized as qualifying income!

### Q2. Can foreign residents or expats enroll in the Youth Jump Account?
Under Article 91-22 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, the Youth Jump Account requires the applicant to be a **Tax Resident of South Korea** who satisfies the NTS income reporting and Resident Registration / Domestic Residence verification via KINFA. Foreign nationals who cannot be verified through the standard Resident Registration household system are generally restricted from the KINFA automated screening, though Marriage Migrants (F-6) and Permanent Residents (F-5) listed on a Korean household register should check eligibility directly through their primary bank's branch counter.

### Q3. What happens if I cannot afford to deposit money for several months?
Because the Youth Jump Account is a **Flexible Installment Savings (자유적립식)** plan, skipping monthly deposits carries **zero penalty** and never cancels your account. You simply do not receive the government matching grant for the specific month you skipped, and can resume depositing up to ₩700,000 whenever your cash flow recovers.
