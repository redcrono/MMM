For global citizens and expat investors residing in South Korea who invest directly in US equities and global ETFs (VOO, QQQ, SCHD, NVDA, TSLA), the primary tax hurdle is Korea's flat **22% Capital Gains Tax (20% national tax + 2% local income tax)**.

While foreign equity capital gains are taxed under a **separate classification (분류과세)**—meaning they do not merge into your employment salary or trigger progressive global income tax brackets—any net realized profit exceeding **2.5 Million KRW per calendar year must be reported to the National Tax Service (NTS) every May**.

This master guide details statutory calculation formulas, currency exchange rate rules, tax-loss harvesting arbitrage, and the T+1 settlement cutoff framework.

## 2. 3 Core Strategies to Minimize or Eliminate Korean Capital Gains Tax

### ① Annual 2.5M KRW Exemption Stepped Basis Reset (Wash Gifting/Rebalancing)
Holding winning positions over a decade causes gains to accumulate into a massive single-year tax liability upon liquidation.
- **Tactic**: Every December, sell positions with unrealized gains up to 2.5M KRW (tax-free under the annual basic deduction) and **immediately repurchase the same shares on the next trading session**.
- **Result**: Your average cost basis resets higher at current market value, permanently eliminating **550,000 KRW (2.5M KRW × 22%) in future capital gains tax every single year**.

### ② Year-End Tax-Loss Harvesting (손익통산)
During high-gain years, sell positions currently sitting at unrealized losses before year-end to offset taxable profits.

## 3. T+1 Settlement Cycle & Year-End Cutoff

In May 2024, US securities markets transitioned to a **T+1 settlement cycle** (next business day settlement).

- **Year-End Trade Deadline**: To ensure trades settle within the current tax year (by December 31), you must execute selling orders at least **1 to 2 business days before the final trading session of December (typically December 29–30)**.
- Orders executing on December 31 will settle on January 2 of the subsequent year, forfeiting the current year's 2.5M KRW deduction.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Will foreign stock profits disqualify my spouse or child from year-end dependent tax deductions?
**Yes! (Critical Cautionary Warning)**
Under Korean tax law, if a dependent (spouse, parent, child) realizes **net foreign stock capital gains exceeding 1,000,000 KRW in a calendar year (before applying the 2.5M KRW basic allowance)**, they are **permanently disqualified as a basic dependent for your Year-End Tax Settlement (1.5M KRW deduction loss)**. Keep trading gains under family accounts below 1M KRW annually.

### Q2. Does NTS automatically aggregate profits across multiple brokerages?
**No.** If you trade across multiple brokerages (e.g., Kiwoom, Toss, Mirae Asset), you must manually combine all profit/loss statements in your May filing. Most Korean brokers offer a free **multi-brokerage consolidated tax filing proxy service** every April.

### Q3. Direct US Shares (VOO/QQQ) vs. Korean-Listed US ETFs (TIGER S&P500): Which is better?
- **Direct US Equities**: 2.5M KRW annual deduction + 22% separate tax (No health insurance premium surcharges, no global income tax).
- **Korean-Listed ETFs**: 15.4% dividend income tax (Unfavorable in general taxable accounts; **massively superior inside ISA and Pension Savings accounts due to tax-free caps and deferred 3.3%–5.5% pension rates**).
