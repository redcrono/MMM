## 1. Progressive Tax (6%–45%) vs 19% Expat Flat Tax: Which Saves You More Money?

Every January and February, all salaried foreign employees in South Korea (`거주자 외국인 근로자`) must complete the **Year-End Tax Settlement (`연말정산`, "13th Month Bonus or Tax Bomb")** through the National Tax Service (NTS) Hometax Simplified Service (`연말정산 간소화 서비스`). During this process, the NTS compares the monthly income tax withheld from your paychecks over the past 12 months against your final calculated tax (`결정세액`), issuing either a cash refund in your February/March paycheck or deducting additional tax.

Under **Article 18-2 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act (조세특례제한법 제18조의2)**, foreign employees enjoy a special privilege for up to **20 years** from the year they first began working in Korea: you can elect to be taxed at a **19% Flat Income Tax Rate (20.9% total including 1.9% local income tax)** on your gross earned income, instead of Korea's standard progressive tax brackets (6% to 45%).

> **`Option A (Progressive Tax) = [(Gross Salary - Earned Income Deduction - Personal/Card/Housing Deductions) × (6%～45% Bracket)] - Tax Credits (Rent, Pension, Medical)`**
>
> **`Option B (19% Expat Flat Tax) = Total Gross Salary (including normally tax-exempt allowances like meals/housing) × 19% (20.9% with local tax) [Zero deductions or tax credits allowed!]`**

**Critical Warning:** If you select the 19% Flat Tax Rate, **all tax-exempt allowances (`비과세 소득`, such as company-paid housing or KRW 200,000 monthly meal allowances), income deductions (`소득공제`), and tax credits (`세액공제`) are completely forfeited** and added back into your taxable base! Because of this rule, choosing the 19% Flat Tax when your salary is KRW 45,000,000 or KRW 70,000,000 will actually cause you to overpay millions of Won in taxes. The 19% Flat Rate only becomes mathematically advantageous when your total annual compensation exceeds **KRW 130,000,000 to KRW 150,000,000+**.

## 3. Two Massive Refunds Expats Miss: Monthly Rent Credit (`월세 세액공제`) & SME Youth 90% Cut

### ① Claiming Up to KRW 1,700,000 Back on Your Monthly Apartment Rent (`월세 세액공제`)
Previously, foreigners were barred from housing deductions because the Resident Registration Act only classified Korean citizens as "Household Heads (`세대주`)." Following major tax law revisions, **foreign tax residents who do not own a home in Korea (`무주택 외국인 거주자`) and earn KRW 80,000,000 or less in gross annual salary** can claim the **Monthly Rent Tax Credit (`월세 세액공제`)**:
- **Gross Salary ≤ KRW 55,000,000:** **17% tax credit** on annual rent (up to KRW 10M limit → max **KRW 1,700,000 cash refund**).
- **Gross Salary KRW 55,000,000 – KRW 80,000,000:** **15% tax credit** on annual rent (max **KRW 1,500,000 cash refund**).
To claim this, your **ARC registered address (`체류지`) must match the address on your Korean lease contract (`임대차계약서`)**, and you must pay rent via bank transfer in your own name. Even if your employer refuses or forgets to process your rent receipt in January, you can claim it privately online via Hometax (`경정청구`, Retroactive Tax Refund Claim) for up to **5 years** without notifying your landlord!

### ② The SME Youth 90% Tax Reduction (`중소기업 취업자 소득세 감면`)
If you are a foreign worker aged **15 to 34** working at a qualifying Korean Small or Medium Enterprise (SME—including most startups, tech firms, restaurants, manufacturing companies, and private academies classified as qualifying industries), you can slash **90% of your Korean income tax (up to KRW 2,000,000 per year) for 5 full years** simply by handing the 1-page application form (`조세특례제한법 별지 제11호 서식`) to your company's accounting office.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Can I claim my parents or spouse living in my home country outside Korea as dependents on my Korean tax return?
**A.** Yes, under strict documentation rules! If your legal spouse (annual income ≤ KRW 1,000,000) or parents aged 60+ (annual income ≤ KRW 1,000,000) live in your home country, you can claim the **KRW 1,500,000 Basic Personal Deduction (`기본공제`)** per person only if you submit: (1) Official government proof of family relationship (apostilled/translated birth or marriage certificate), (2) Official home-country tax authority proof that their annual income was under KRW 1,000,000, and (3) **Overseas Remittance Receipts (`해외송금영수증`) proving you regularly wired living expenses to support them**. (Note: Medical, education, and credit card expenses spent by dependents living abroad are never deductible.)

### Q2. I am a freelancer or independent contractor paying the 3.3% withholding tax (`3.3% 사업소득자`). Why didn't my company do Year-End Tax Settlement for me in January?
**A.** If your employer deducts a flat **3.3% tax (3% business income tax + 0.3% local tax)** from your pay, you are classified under Korean tax law as an **Independent Business Contractor (`인적용역 사업소득자`)**, not a salaried employee (`근로소득자`). Independent contractors do **not** participate in the January/February Year-End Tax Settlement. Instead, you **must file your own Comprehensive Income Tax Return (`종합소득세 신고`) on Hometax between May 1 and May 31** every year. For most 3.3% expat contractors earning under KRW 40,000,000, filing in May triggers a substantial cash refund of the 3.3% tax withheld during the previous year!

### Q3. Can I open a Korean Pension Savings (`연금저축`) or IRP account for the KRW 1,485,000 tax credit if I plan to leave Korea in 2 or 3 years?
**A.** Be very careful here: while contributing KRW 6,000,000–9,000,000 into a Korean Pension Savings or IRP account gives you a **16.5% tax refund** (if salary ≤ KRW 55M) or **13.2% tax refund** (if salary > KRW 55M) in February, closing the account before age 55 when you leave Korea triggers a **16.5% Early Termination Other Income Tax (`기타소득세 16.5%`)** on the deducted principal and investment gains! Unless you qualify for the **Permanent Emigration Exception (`해외이주 사유 저율 과세`)** or earn over KRW 55,000,000 and plan to invest the tax-deferred refund for several years, short-term expats staying under 3 years are better off using a **Korean ISA account (3-year maturity)** or claiming the **Monthly Rent Tax Credit (`월세 세액공제`)**.
