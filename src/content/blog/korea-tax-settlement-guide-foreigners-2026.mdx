If you are an expatriate, foreign engineer, teacher, or international professional working in South Korea, every January brings the country's most significant personal finance event: <strong>Yeonmaljeongsan (연말정산 - Year-End Tax Settlement)</strong>.

Commonly referred to as the <strong>"13th Month Salary"</strong>, doing your tax settlement correctly can yield a direct bank refund of 1,000,000 KRW to 3,000,000+ KRW in February.

---

## 1. The Big Choice: 19% Flat Tax Rate vs. General Progressive Tax Rate

Foreign employees in Korea enjoy a unique tax benefit: the option to choose between the standard progressive tax system and a <strong>19% flat tax rate</strong>.

## 2. Key Deductions Foreigners Frequently Miss

1. <strong>Credit and Debit Card Usage:</strong> Spending exceeding 25% of your gross annual salary qualifies for deductions (15% for credit cards, 30% for check cards/cash receipts).
2. <strong>Monthly Rent Tax Credit (Wolse):</strong> Foreign workers who are registered residents and meet income thresholds can claim up to 17% tax credit on rent paid.
3. <strong>Medical Expenses:</strong> Qualified medical bills in excess of 3% of your annual salary are eligible for a 15% tax credit.
