The most paradoxical financial development currently rattling South Korea's credit markets is that **the Bank of Korea held its base rate steady at 2.50%, yet mortgage interest rates at commercial bank counters surged past 6.0%**.

With national household debt surpassing an unprecedented **2,000 Trillion KRW ($1.5 Trillion)**, Korean financial authorities issued strict directives warning top commercial lenders (KB, Shinhan, Hana, Woori, NH) to keep annual loan growth below a tight **1.5% ceiling**. To suppress surging borrowing demand, banks retaliated by **hiking their discretionary lending spreads (가산금리) 5 to 6 consecutive times in under 60 days**.

Furthermore, lenders abruptly halted **"Conditional Jeonse Loans" (preventing gap investment transfers)** and clamped down on online refinancing platforms, triggering an acute credit squeeze across the capital region.

## 1. 2.50% Base Rate vs. 6.0% Mortgage: How Did This Divergence Happen?

To understand how borrowing costs spiked while monetary policy remained paused, we must examine the Korean mortgage pricing formula:

> 📌 **[Korean Mortgage Pricing Formula]**  
> **Final Mortgage Rate** = **Benchmark Yield (5-Year Bank Bond or COFIX)** + **Bank Add-on Spread** - **Relationship Discounts**

Under normal market conditions, softening inflation pulls benchmark bond yields down, lowering loan rates. However, facing strict regulatory loan caps, commercial banks systematically **raised their internal profit spreads by +0.50%p each time benchmark yields dipped by 0.20%p**, engineering a deliberate rate surge.

## 2. The 3 Regulatory Roadblocks Facing Borrowers

### ① Suspension of Ownership-Transfer Conditional Jeonse Loans
- **The Mechanism**: In Korea, gap investors frequently sign a purchase agreement and simultaneously recruit a tenant whose Jeonse deposit funds the seller's final balance. Banks are now **refusing all Jeonse loans executed on the exact date of ownership transfer**.
- **Market Impact**: Genuine homebuyers attempting to upgrade homes and landlords needing to return deposits face severe liquidity freezes.

### ② Mortgage Tenure Curtailment (40 Years ➔ 30 Years)
- **The Mechanism**: 40-to-50-year amortizations allowed borrowers to stretch principal payments, artificially lowering annual DSR ratios.
- **Market Impact**: Reducing loan tenure to 30 years **erodes borrowing capacity by approximately 65 Million KRW ($48,000) for an 80M KRW salary earner**.

### ③ Restriction of Mortgage Credit Insurance (MCI / MCG 방공제)
- **The Mechanism**: Banks no longer absorb the statutory Small-Scale Tenant Protection Deposit (55M KRW in Seoul, 48M KRW in Gyeonggi).
- **Market Impact**: The maximum loan disbursement is **automatically reduced by 55 Million KRW** across all Seoul property applications.
