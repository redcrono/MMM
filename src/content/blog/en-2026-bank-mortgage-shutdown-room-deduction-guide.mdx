A severe credit crunch is hitting South Korea's real estate market ahead of the peak fall relocation season. <strong>The nation's top five commercial lenders (KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Hana, Woori, and NH NongHyup) have effectively initiated a 'mortgage shutdown'</strong> after approaching their annual regulatory household debt growth ceilings.

The most critical operational shift is the <strong>suspension of Mortgage Credit Insurance (MCI & MCG)</strong>. By blocking mortgage insurance enrollments, banks are now legally required to deduct statutory small-tenant priority security deposits—known as the <strong>'Room Deduction' (₩55 Million in Seoul, ₩48 Million in Gyeonggi/Incheon)—directly from maximum loan limits</strong>.

Compounded by suspensions of loan broker origination, rapid exhaustion of digital daily quotas, and bans on conditional leasehold (Jeonse) financing, thousands of home buyers who signed purchase contracts now face sudden settlement financing shortfalls.

This briefing analyzes the mechanics of commercial bank mortgage restrictions, models the financial impact of the ₩55M room deduction, and outlines 3 actionable financing workarounds for homebuyers.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Is the room deduction applied to every bedroom?
For apartments, the statutory deduction is limited to one room per household (₩55M in Seoul, ₩48M in Gyeonggi/Incheon). For multi-family villas, deductions are applied per room, resulting in much steeper borrowing limits.

### Q2. Can existing single-home owners upgrade by selling their current home?
Some commercial banks make exceptions for existing 1-home owners who sign formal disposal agreements and submit verified sales contracts before closing.

### Q3. What if a loan is rejected after earnest money is paid?
Unless a protective contract rider ("Contract nullified and earnest money returned if mortgage is denied without buyer's fault") was negotiated, the deposit may be forfeited. Borrowers should immediately seek non-bank insurer loan approvals.
