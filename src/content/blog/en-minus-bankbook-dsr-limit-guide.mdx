Revolving overdraft credit lines, known colloquially in South Korea as **"Minus Bankbooks (마이너스통장)"**, are widely favored by corporate employees for emergency liquidity because interest is assessed strictly on drawn daily balances.

However, prospective real estate homebuyers and mortgage applicants often encounter a devastating surprise during bank underwriting: **holding an unused 50 Million KRW overdraft line with a zero balance can wipe out over 140 Million to 260 Million KRW in mortgage borrowing capacity**.

Under FSC and FSS Debt Service Ratio (DSR) regulations, banks **assess 100% of the approved credit limit as active amortizing debt across a mandatory 5-year assumed term**.

This master guide evaluates statutory DSR formulas, comparative borrowing simulations, Tier-1 vs. Tier-2 overdraft structures, and the golden sequencing of borrowing.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Does cancelling an overdraft line restore DSR capacity on the same day?
**Yes.** Once you execute a line cancellation in your banking app, the bank transmits real-time cancellation data to the Korea Credit Information Services (KCIS), restoring your DSR capacity instantly.

### Q2. What happens if I keep an overdraft line open for a full year without drawing funds?
Under standard banking terms, accounts with zero drawing activity over 12 consecutive months may face a **mandatory 10%–20% limit reduction or loss of prime interest rate discounts upon annual renewal**.

### Q3. Can foreign residents open overdraft lines in Korea?
**Yes.** Foreign residents holding professional visas (E-7, D-8, F-2, F-4, F-5, F-6) with verifiable Korean W-2 earned income (근로소득원천징수영수증) are eligible to open overdraft facilities at major Korean commercial banks.
