## 1. How South Korea Taxes Overseas Equity Capital Gains

For domestic retail investors ("Seohak Ants") and foreign expats who qualify as tax residents in South Korea (having a domicile in Korea for 183 days or more, or over 5 years of cumulative residence for foreign nationals under the Foreign Tax Resident exception), profits earned from selling overseas stocks—such as US-listed S&P 500 ETFs (`VOO`, `SPY`), Nasdaq-100 ETFs (`QQQ`), or individual tech equities (`NVDA`, `AAPL`, `MSFT`)—are classified as foreign asset capital gains.

Unlike domestic KOSPI or KOSDAQ shares, which remain exempt from capital gains tax for retail investors following the abolition of the Financial Investment Income Tax (금투세 폐지), overseas equities are strictly taxed on a calendar-year realized basis (January 1 through December 31).

> **`Final Capital Gains Tax = (∑ Realized Gains - ∑ Realized Losses - ₩2,500,000 Basic Deduction - Brokerage Fees) × 22%`**

Crucially, the National Tax Service (NTS) calculates your gain or loss in **Korean Won (KRW)** using the base exchange rate (매매기준율) on the **settlement date**, not the trade execution date. This means even if a US stock's dollar price remains flat, a sharp depreciation of the Korean Won between your purchase date and sale date can generate a taxable FX capital gain in KRW terms.

### Strategy 1: Annual ₩2.5M Capital Gain Harvesting (Cost-Basis Step-Up)
Because the ₩2,500,000 annual basic deduction evaporates on December 31 if unused, long-term buy-and-hold investors should never let a calendar year pass without realizing at least ₩2.5M in profits. By selling just enough shares of an appreciated ETF (e.g., `QQQ`) to realize ₩2,500,000 in gain and immediately repurchasing those shares on the same trading night, you permanently raise your average acquisition cost basis and save **₩550,000** in future taxes—minus roughly ₩10,000 in round-trip trading commissions.

### Strategy 2: December Tax-Loss Harvesting Without Wash-Sale Penalties
If you realized ₩15,000,000 in profits earlier in the year on semiconductor stocks (`NVDA`) and currently hold a ₩12,500,000 unrealized loss in secondary battery or biotech stocks, holding the losers past December 31 forces you to pay `((₩15M - ₩2.5M) × 22%) = ₩2,750,000` in tax next May.
Instead, selling the losing position in late December realizes the ₩12.5M loss, reducing your net annual gain to exactly ₩2.5M (₩0 tax liability). If you still believe in the long-term thesis of the losing stock, you can repurchase it 30 seconds later—Korea's tax code has **no wash-sale waiting period**.

## 3. Critical Pitfall: Moving Average vs. FIFO Accounting Across Brokers

One of the most expensive mistakes retail investors make during December tax-loss harvesting is failing to check how their Korean brokerage calculates cost basis:
- **Moving Average Method (이동평균법):** Used by Kiwoom Securities, Toss Securities, NH Investment & Securities, and KB Securities. Every purchase blends into a single average cost price per share.
- **First-In, First-Out (선입선출법 - FIFO):** Used by Samsung Securities, Mirae Asset Securities, and Korea Investment & Securities. When you sell a partial position, the earliest shares purchased are sold first.

If you bought `TSLA` at $150 in 2023 and added more at $380 in late 2025, your overall dashboard in a FIFO brokerage might show a modest gain, yet selling 30% of your holdings will liquidate the $150 shares first—triggering a massive unexpected capital gain rather than a loss! Always check the **"Overseas Stock Capital Gains Tax Simulation (해외주식 양도소득세 가계산)"** screen inside your brokerage app before executing December trades.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. If I stay under the ₩2.5M annual profit threshold, do I still have to file a return in May?
**No.** If your net realized overseas equity gain across all brokerages for the calendar year is ₩2,500,000 or less, your payable tax is ₩0 and you have no legal obligation to file a capital gains tax return with the National Tax Service.

### Q2. Does realizing ₩2.5M in US stock gains disqualify my spouse as a tax dependent?
**Yes, if net income exceeds ₩1,000,000!** This is a notorious trap. While the *capital gains tax exemption* is ₩2.5M, the **basic dependent deduction (인적공제 부양가족 요건)** in Korea's Year-End Tax Settlement caps a dependent's total annual income (including realized overseas stock gains) at **₩1,000,000**. If your non-working spouse realizes ₩2.4M in US stock gains, they pay ₩0 capital gains tax, but you lose their ₩1.5M personal deduction and all their medical/credit card deductions on your salary tax return! Keep dependents' realized US stock profits under **₩1,000,000**.

### Q3. Do US stock capital gains increase my National Health Insurance (NHIS) premiums?
**No.** In South Korea, National Health Insurance premiums (건강보험료) and the ₩20M dependent disqualification threshold are assessed on interest, dividends, business, pension, and earned income—**not** on capital gains (양도소득). Even if you realize ₩100 million in US stock capital gains, your NHIS premium and dependent status remain completely unaffected.
