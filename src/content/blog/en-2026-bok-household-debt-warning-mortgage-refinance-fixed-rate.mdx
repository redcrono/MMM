On October 12, 2026, the Bank of Korea (BOK) issued a strong warning during its parliamentary report, emphasizing that monetary tightening and strict household debt management will persist to rein in real estate speculation and structural leverage.

With global higher-for-longer rate pressures spilling into Korea's commercial lending market, the most vulnerable borrowers are **those still holding 4.7%–5.0% floating-rate mortgages**. Betting on rapid rate cuts while remaining in variable loans risks leaking hundreds of thousands of won every month in avoidable interest drag.

Under regulatory pressure, commercial banks have introduced **5-year periodic fixed-rate mortgages in the 3.4%–3.7% range**—creating a rare inversion where fixed rates are significantly cheaper than variable COFIX products. Here is the actionable 3-step blueprint to lock in savings before windows close.

## 2. 3 Golden Rules Before Refinancing

1. **Stress DSR Phase 2/3 Exemption:**
   - 5-year periodic fixed-rate loans receive minimal or zero stress rate surcharges under Korean regulations, preventing loan amount reductions during refinancing.
2. **The 3-Year Threshold (Zero Penalty):**
   - By Korean law, if your existing mortgage is over 3 years old, early repayment fees are **strictly 0 KRW**. It is a 100% friction-free switch.
3. **LTV & KB Real Estate Valuation:**
   - Ensure your property's latest KB market price maintains loan-to-value (LTV) limits before applying.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What if the BOK cuts rates in the future?**  
A. Market expectations for cuts are already priced into bond yields, which is why fixed rates are currently much lower than variable rates. Furthermore, 5-year periodic products reset after 5 years, providing an optimal hedge against near-term volatility.

**Q2. Does refinancing hurt my credit score?**  
A. No. In fact, lower monthly repayments decrease your DSR burden, which frequently improves your KCB and NICE credit rating scores.
