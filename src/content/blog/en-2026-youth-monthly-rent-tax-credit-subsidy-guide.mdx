To alleviate mounting urban housing burdens for young workers and foreign professionals residing in Korea, the government officially announced the <strong>expansion of the Youth Monthly Rent Tax Credit ceiling from ₩10 Million to ₩12 Million</strong>, enacting a <strong>flat 17% tax credit rate for young tenants aged 15 to 34 (delivering annual tax refunds up to ₩2.04 Million / ~$1,500 USD)</strong>.

Concurrently, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport maintains the <strong>'Special Youth Monthly Rent Subsidy' (paying ₩200,000 per month in direct non-taxable cash for up to 24 months, totaling ₩4.8 Million)</strong> via the Bokjiro portal.

However, many eligible tenants miss out on these statutory benefits due to misconceptions surrounding <strong>landlord consent, studio/officetel eligibility, and combining government cash subsidies with year-end tax credits</strong>.

This guide breaks down the statutory mechanics of the ₩12M rent tax credit, contrasts it against Bokjiro direct subsidies, models annual refund payouts, and outlines a 3-step application roadmap.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Is landlord consent or signature required for the tax credit?
<strong>No.</strong> Rent tax credits are filed directly with the National Tax Service (NTS). No landlord consent or notification is involved, protecting tenant privacy.

### Q2. Are officetels, studios, and gosiwons eligible?
Yes. Residential officetels, studio apartments, and registered gosiwons are all fully eligible provided the tenant's name is on the lease and resident registration is completed.

### Q3. Can foreign residents and expats claim the rent tax credit?
Yes. Registered foreign residents paying Korean wage income tax are eligible for housing rent deductions under identical statutory criteria as Korean nationals.
