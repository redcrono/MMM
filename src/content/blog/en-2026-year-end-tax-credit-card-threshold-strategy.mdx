## 1. How the 25% Gross Salary Threshold Works in Korea's Tax Code

Every October and November, the National Tax Service (NTS) opens the **Year-End Tax Settlement Preview Service (연말정산 미리보기)** on HomeTax, summarizing your cumulative credit card, debit card, and cash receipt spending from January through September. Understanding the mathematical mechanics of the **Credit Card Income Deduction (신용카드 등 사용금액 소득공제)** during Q4 can easily swing your February paycheck by **₩300,000 to ₩800,000**.

First, no matter which payment method you use, the **first 25% of your Gross Salary (총급여액)** yields **₩0 tax deduction**. Crucially, the National Tax Service automatically fills this 25% hurdle using your **lowest-deduction-rate spending first (Credit Cards at 15%)**!

> **`Deductible Card Amount = ( Credit Card (excess) × 15% ) + ( Check Card / Cash Receipt × 30% ) + ( Traditional Market × 40% ) + ( Public Transit × 80% )`**

For example, if your annual gross salary is **₩60,000,000**, your 25% minimum hurdle is **₩15,000,000**:
- **First ₩15,000,000 of annual spending:** Put 100% of this on **Credit Cards** (which pay 1.5%–3.0% cashback or airline miles). Because the first ₩15M gets zero tax deduction anyway, using a debit card here wastes credit card rewards!
- **Every Won spent above ₩15,000,000:** Switch immediately to **Check/Debit Cards, ZeroPay, or Cash Receipts (현금영수증)**, which earn a **30% tax deduction rate**—double the 15% rate of credit cards!

## 3. Dual-Income Couples (맞벌이 부부): Which Card Should You Use in Q4?

For married dual-income couples, choosing whose card to swipe during November and December depends on where each spouse stands relative to the 25% threshold:
1. **Scenario A (One Spouse Can Easily Clear 25%, the Other Cannot):**
   Suppose Spouse A earns ₩80M (25% hurdle = ₩20M) and Spouse B earns ₩40M (25% hurdle = ₩10M), and the household spends ₩22M per year on cards. If they split spending ₩11M each, Spouse A gets **₩0** deduction and Spouse B only gets a deduction on ₩1M. Instead, concentrating **all ₩22M onto Spouse B's card** clears the ₩10M hurdle early and generates a tax deduction on **₩12,000,000** of spending!
2. **Scenario B (Both Spouses Have Already Cleared Their 25% Hurdle):**
   Once both spouses exceed 25% of their respective salaries, shift remaining Q4 spending onto the card of the **higher-earning spouse** (who sits in a higher marginal income tax bracket, e.g., 26.4% vs. 16.5%) until their ₩2.5M/₩3.0M base deduction ceiling is full.

## 4. What Does NOT Count Toward the Card Tax Deduction (Double-Dip Rules)

Not every card swipe is recognized by the National Tax Service. However, several major categories allow **Double Dipping (중복 공제)**—meaning you get **both** the 15%–30% Card Income Deduction **and** a special Tax Credit!
- **100% Excluded from Card Deduction:** New car purchases (신차 구입비 - though **used cars** count up to 10% of purchase price!), national and local taxes, utility bills (electricity, water, gas), apartment management fees, highway tolls, insurance premiums, overseas duty-free/foreign card spending, and **kindergarten/school/university tuition**.
- **Allowed Double-Dipping (Card Deduction + Tax Credit Simultaneously):**
  1. **Medical Expenses (의료비):** Hospital, pharmacy, dental, and LASIK/orthodontic bills paid by card earn **both** the 15%/30% card deduction and the 15% Medical Tax Credit!
  2. **Preschool Private Academy Fees (취학 전 아동 학원비):** Art, music, or sports academy fees for children **before elementary school entry** earn **both** the card deduction and the 15% Education Tax Credit!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. If I pay a ₩3,000,000 hospital bill on a 6-month credit card installment in December, which year gets the deduction?
Under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, credit card installment purchases (할부 결제) are deducted in the tax year of the **initial purchase approval date (결제 승인일 기준)**, not when you pay the monthly card bill! Therefore, a ₩3,000,000 purchase made on December 28 on a 6-month installment counts 100% toward your **current year's** tax return.

### Q2. Can foreign workers on the 19% Flat Tax Rate (외국인 단일세율) claim the Credit Card Income Deduction?
**No.** Foreign employees in South Korea who elect the special **19% Flat Income Tax Rate (20.9% including local tax)** waive all income deductions and tax credits, including the credit card deduction. However, foreign employees taxed under the **standard progressive tax brackets (6%–45%)** qualify for the exact same credit/debit card, traditional market, and public transit deductions as Korean citizens.

### Q3. Can I claim my spouse's credit card spending if my spouse earned ₩1,200,000 in net income this year?
**No.** You can only combine a spouse's or parent/child's card spending onto your tax return if that family member qualifies as a **Basic Deduction Dependent (기본공제 대상자)**—meaning their annual net income amount is **₩1,000,000 or less** (or gross salary ₩5,000,000 or less if they have only earned income). Note that siblings' (형제자매) card spending can **never** be claimed, even if they live with you as dependents.
