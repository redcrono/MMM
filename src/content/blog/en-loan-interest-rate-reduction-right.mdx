If your financial health has improved following a corporate promotion, salary raise, credit score increase, or debt payoff since taking out a loan in South Korea, you possess the enforceable legal right to mandate that your bank lower your loan interest rate: the **Right to Request Interest Rate Reduction (금리인하요구권)** under Article 45-2 of the Financial Consumer Protection Act.

Korean commercial banks are statutorily required to **adjudicate and formally notify applicants of their review decision within 10 business days**.

On a 400 Million KRW mortgage, a 1.40%p interest reduction saves **5,600,000 KRW annually (28 Million KRW over 5 years)** in direct cash interest.

This master guide details the 5 legal qualification criteria, ineligible loan products, and the mobile banking application protocol.

## 3. Eligible vs. Ineligible Loan Facilities

- **✅ Eligible Loans**: Standard bank credit loans, minus checking credit lines, commercial apartment mortgages, and Jeonse rental loans where borrower creditworthiness determines bank spread margins.
- **❌ Ineligible Loans**: Sovereign policy mortgages (Didimdol, Beotimmok, Newborn Special Loans, Bogeumjari Loans) and deposit-collateralized loans where rates are legally fixed.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. How many times per year can I submit a rate reduction request?
**There is zero statutory frequency limit.** You can submit requests whenever your income rises or credit score improves—whether twice or three times in a single year.

### Q2. Does getting rejected hurt my credit score?
**No.** Rate reduction inquiries and adjudications are strictly internal bank administrative procedures and carry **zero impact on KCB/NICE credit scoring**.

### Q3. Can foreign residents holding an ARC exercise this right?
**Yes.** All foreign residents holding personal or mortgage loans at South Korean banking institutions enjoy identical statutory rights under the Financial Consumer Protection Act.
