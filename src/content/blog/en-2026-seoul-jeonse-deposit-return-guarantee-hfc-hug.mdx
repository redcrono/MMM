## 1. Understanding the "126% Rule" Before Signing Any Jeonse Lease

In South Korea's unique **Jeonse (전세)** lump-sum deposit lease system, your entire life savings can be wiped out if a landlord becomes insolvent or an underwater villa ("깡통전세") goes to court auction. To protect your deposit, enrolling in a **Jeonse Deposit Return Guarantee (전세보증금반환보증)** through the Korea Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation (**HUG**), Korea Housing Finance Corporation (**HF**), or Seoul Guarantee Insurance (**SGI**) guarantees that the state agency pays back 100% of your deposit if the landlord fails to return it at lease maturity.

However, following nationwide Jeonse fraud crackdowns, HUG and HF tightened their **Property Valuation & LTV Cap**:
- **Recognized Property Value:** `Official Published Price (공동주택/단독주택 공시가격) × 140%`
- **Maximum Guaranteed Deposit Ratio:** `Recognized Property Value × 90%`

$$\text{Maximum Insurable Jeonse Deposit} = (\text{Official Published Price} \times 140\% \times 90\%) - \text{Senior Mortgages (선순위 채권)} = \text{Published Price} \times 126\% - \text{Senior Debt}$$

For example, if a multi-family villa in Mapo-gu has an Official Published Price of **₩200,000,000** and zero landlord mortgage, the maximum Jeonse deposit eligible for HUG guarantee insurance is exactly **₩252,000,000** (`₩200M × 1.26`). If the landlord demands ₩260,000,000, HUG will **reject 100% of your insurance application**! Always check the property's published price on `realtyprice.kr` or the **HUG Safe Jeonse App (안심전세 App)** before wiring a contract deposit.

## 3. Claim Your Up to ₩300,000 Government Guarantee Fee Refund!

To encourage every tenant to insure their deposit, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport and all 17 provincial/metropolitan governments operate the **Jeonse Deposit Return Guarantee Fee Support Program (전세보증금반환보증 보증료 지원사업)**, refunding up to **₩300,000** in cash directly to your bank account once you enroll in HUG, HF, or SGI insurance:
- **Deposit Limit:** Jeonse deposit of **₩300,000,000 or less** nationwide.
- **Income Limits (Homeless Tenants):**
  - **Youth (Aged 19–39):** Annual income **≤ ₩50,000,000** (100% of fee refunded up to ₩300,000).
  - **General Citizens (All Ages):** Annual income **≤ ₩60,000,000** (90% of fee refunded up to ₩300,000).
  - **Newlyweds (Married within 7 years):** Combined couple income **≤ ₩75,000,000** (100% of fee refunded up to ₩300,000).

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Do I need the landlord's permission or signature to enroll in HUG Jeonse Guarantee Insurance?
**No!** You never need your landlord's consent or signature to enroll in HUG, HF, or SGI Jeonse Deposit Return Guarantee insurance. As long as your lease contract was signed through a licensed real estate agent (공인중개사) and you have completed your Move-In Report and Fixed Date, you can enroll unilaterally online.

### Q2. Can foreign nationals (E-7, D-2, F-2, F-4, F-5, F-6 visa holders) enroll in HUG Jeonse Guarantee Insurance?
**Yes!** Under Article 88 of the Immigration Act and the Housing Lease Protection Act, when a registered foreign national files a **Change of Place of Stay (체류지변경신고)** or **Domestic Residence Report (거소신고 for F-4 holders)** at their local district office/community center and obtains a **Fixed Date (확정일자)** on their lease contract, it carries the exact same legal opposing power (대항력) and priority repayment right (우선변제권) as a Korean citizen's move-in report, making them fully eligible for HUG and SGI deposit guarantees.

### Q3. What should I do if the official published price × 126% is slightly lower than my landlord's asking Jeonse price?
You have two legal options:
1. **Negotiate a Semi-Jeonse (반전세) Split:** Lower the lump-sum Jeonse deposit to fit strictly inside the `126%` HUG limit, and convert the difference into a small monthly rent.
2. **Request a HUG Certified Appraisal (인정감정평가):** If recent market sales prove the property is worth significantly more than the outdated published price, the landlord or tenant can request a formal appraisal through a HUG-designated appraisal firm (though you must weigh the appraisal fee first).
