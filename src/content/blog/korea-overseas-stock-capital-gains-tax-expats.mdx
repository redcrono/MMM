## 1. The Most Misunderstood Tax Rule for Expats: The 5-Year Foreign Resident Rule

Thousands of foreign professionals, engineers, teachers, and executives living in South Korea invest in US stocks, ETFs (such as S&P 500 or QQQ), or global equities—either through an offshore brokerage in their home country (Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, Vanguard, Trading 212) or through a domestic Korean brokerage app (Mirae Asset, NH Investment, Toss Securities).

Before calculating your 22% Korean capital gains tax (`해외주식 양도소득세`), you must first determine which of the **three legal tax statuses** you occupy under the **South Korean Income Tax Act (소득세법 제1조의2 및 제118조의2)**:

1. **Non-Resident (`비거주자`, staying in Korea < 183 days without domestic domicile):** Taxed by Korea *only* on Korean-sourced income. Zero Korean tax on overseas stocks.
2. **Short-to-Medium Term Foreign Tax Resident (`단기 외국인 거주자`, Korean tax resident whose total period of domicile/residence in Korea during the past 10 years is 5 years [60 months] or less):** Under **Article 3 and Article 118-2 of the Income Tax Act**, for foreign-source assets (such as US stocks or foreign real estate), you are taxed by South Korea **ONLY if the income/proceeds are paid in Korea (`국내에서 지급`) or remitted into Korea (`국내로 송금`)**.
3. **Long-Term Foreign Tax Resident (`장기 외국인 거주자`, residing in Korea > 5 years out of the past 10 years):** Subject to full worldwide taxation on overseas stock capital gains regardless of whether you trade on a US brokerage or a Korean brokerage, and regardless of whether you remit the money to Korea.

> **`Korean Overseas Stock Capital Gains Tax (For >5-Year Residents or Domestic Brokerage Traders) = [Annual Net Realized Gain in KRW (converted at settlement-date exchange rates) - KRW 2,500,000 Basic Deduction] × 22%`**

## 3. Foreign Exchange (KRW) Calculation Trap & Tax-Loss Harvesting for Expats

If you trade US stocks through a Korean securities firm (or if you have lived in Korea for more than 5 years), you must master two essential tax mechanics before December 31:

### ① The Settlement-Date KRW Exchange Rate Rule (`결제일 기준환율 원칙`)
The Korean National Tax Service (NTS) calculates capital gains strictly in **Korean Won (KRW)** based on the basic exchange rate (`기준환율`) on the **T+1 settlement date of purchase** versus the **T+1 settlement date of sale**—not in US Dollars.
For example, if you bought USD 20,000 of Apple stock when USD/KRW was 1,250 (cost basis: KRW 25,000,000) and sold it for the exact same USD 20,000 when USD/KRW rose to 1,400 (sale proceeds: KRW 28,000,000), your USD profit is $0, but the Korean NTS assesses a **KRW 3,000,000 currency capital gain**! After subtracting the KRW 2,500,000 basic deduction, you would owe 22% on the remaining KRW 500,000.

### ② Tax-Loss Harvesting (Zero Wash-Sale Restriction in Korea!)
Unlike the US IRS (which enforces a 30-day Wash Sale Rule disallowing loss deductions if you repurchase the same stock within 30 days), **South Korean tax law has NO wash-sale restriction on overseas stocks**.
If you have realized KRW 10,000,000 in gains this year and hold another US stock sitting at an unrealized loss of KRW -6,000,000, you can sell the losing position before the final US settlement day in December (December 30 for T+1 settlement) to lock in the KRW -6,000,000 loss, reducing your net taxable gain to KRW 4,000,000—and **repurchase your shares 1 minute later** if you are not a US taxpayer subject to IRS wash-sale rules.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Does realizing overseas stock capital gains affect my Korean National Health Insurance (NHIS) premiums?
**A.** **No!** Under South Korea's National Health Insurance Act, **capital gains (`양도소득`) are completely excluded** from the income assessment used to calculate NHIS health insurance premiums (`건강보험료`) and dependent eligibility (`피부양자 자격`). Even if you realize KRW 50,000,000 in US stock capital gains and pay the 22% capital gains tax in May, your monthly NHIS health insurance bill will not increase by a single Won. (By contrast, **dividend income `배당소득` and interest income `이자소득`** exceeding KRW 20,000,000 per year *do* trigger extra NHIS surcharges and strip dependent status.)

### Q2. What happens if I earn less than KRW 2,500,000 in net overseas stock profit for the year? Do I still need to file in May?
**A.** If your net realized gain across all overseas stock trades between January 1 and December 31 is **KRW 2,500,000 or less**, your taxable base after the basic deduction is zero (`KRW 0`), and you have **no legal obligation to file a capital gains tax return** in May.

### Q3. Why should long-term expats in Korea consider investing via a Korean ISA (Individual Savings Account) instead of direct US brokerage accounts?
**A.** Once you qualify as a Korean tax resident (with earned or business income in Korea), you can open a **Korean Brokerage ISA (`중개형 ISA`)**. Inside an ISA, you can buy Korean-listed US index ETFs (such as `TIGER 미국S&P500` or `KODEX 미국나스닥100`). Unlike direct US stock investing (22% tax above KRW 2.5M), an ISA gives you **KRW 2,000,000 to KRW 4,000,000 in completely tax-free gains**, and any profits above that threshold are taxed at a **low 9.9% separated rate** at maturity—with full loss offsetting and zero impact on your NHIS health insurance premiums.
