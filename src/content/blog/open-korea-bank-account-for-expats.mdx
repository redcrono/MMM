## 1. Understanding South Korea's Strict Anti-Fraud Banking Rules for Expats

For any foreign professional (E-2, E-7), international student (D-2, D-4), or long-term resident (F-2, F-4, F-5, F-6) arriving in South Korea, opening a domestic Korean bank account (`입출금 통장`) is the gateway to survival. Without a Korean bank account tied to your **Residence Card (ARC, 외국인등록증)**, you cannot verify your identity via PASS/Kakao authentication, receive your monthly salary, pay housing rent (`월세`), or link domestic shopping apps like Coupang and Naver Pay.

However, under strict Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) anti-voice-phishing regulations, Korean banks enforce the **Proof of Financial Purpose Rule (금융거래목적 확인제도)**. Even after you wait 3 to 4 weeks for Korean Immigration to issue your physical ARC, walking into a bank branch with just your passport and ARC will only grant you a **Restricted Limit Account (`한도제한계좌`)**.

> **`Restricted Account Daily Transfer Cap = KRW 300,000/day (Mobile App & Internet Banking) + KRW 300,000/day (ATM Withdrawal) + KRW 1,000,000/day (In-Person Branch Teller)`**

Because monthly apartment rent in Seoul easily ranges from KRW 600,000 to KRW 1,500,000+, a KRW 300,000 daily transfer limit makes paying your landlord impossible in a single transaction unless you either lift the restriction (`한도제한 해제`) or register a verified recurring auto-transfer (`자동이체`) at the teller window.

## 3. How to Lift the KRW 300,000 Daily Transfer Limit (`한도제한 해제`)

To upgrade your Restricted Account to a **Normal Uncapped Account (`일반 계좌`)**—which raises your mobile transfer limit to KRW 10,000,000–100,000,000 per day—you must satisfy one of the bank's official verification tracks:

1. **The Employer Payroll Track (Fastest for E-2/E-7/F-visa Workers):** Bring your **Certificate of Employment (`재직증명서`)** stamped with the company seal, your **Employment Contract (`근로계약서`)**, and either your first **Salary Payslip (`급여명세서`)** or your **NHIS Workplace Subscriber Qualification Certificate (`건강보험 자격득실확인서`)**. If your employer uses that specific bank as its corporate payroll partner (`주거래은행`), the teller will lift the limit on Day 1.
2. **The Housing Lease Workaround (For Paying Monthly Rent Before Lifting the Cap):** If you are a student (D-2/D-4) or job seeker (D-10) who cannot lift the cap immediately, bring your **notarized Korean Apartment Lease Contract (`임대차계약서`)** to the bank branch. The teller can register a **Designated Landlord Auto-Transfer (`월세 자동이체 등록`)** that bypasses the KRW 300,000 cap every month, or process a teller-assisted transfer up to KRW 1,000,000/day.
3. **The 3-Month Utility/Payroll History Track:** Once your account shows **3 consecutive months** of salary deposits (`급여 입금`) OR 3 consecutive months of automatic utility/telecom/apartment management fee debits (`공과금·통신비 자동이체`), you can request immediate limit removal inside the bank app or at any branch.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Can I open a Korean bank account on a 90-day Tourist Visa (K-ETA / Visa-Free) without an ARC?
**A.** As of 2026, almost all major Korean banks strictly prohibit opening standard domestic checking accounts or issuing mobile banking access on a short-term tourist visa without an ARC and a domestic residential address. While a few airport/foreign-exchange branches technically allow passport-only non-resident passbook accounts (`비거주자 원화계좌`), they come with zero mobile banking, zero check card, and require in-person teller visits for every withdrawal. Short-term visitors should use **WOWPASS** or **NAMANE Card** instead.

### Q2. Why does online identity verification (본인인증) keep failing even after I opened my bank account?
**A.** 99% of Korean identity verification failures happen because of a **character mismatch between your Telecom registration and your Bank registration**. Korean systems treat spaces, hyphens, and lowercase letters as different characters. Your name must appear in the exact same order, all-caps, without hyphens, as printed on the back/front of your ARC: **`SURNAME GIVENNAME MIDDLENAME`** (e.g., `SMITH JOHN DAVID`). If your bank teller typed `JOHN DAVID SMITH` or omitted your middle name, visit the branch with your ARC to synchronize your official profile name.

### Q3. How much money can a foreigner legally wire out of South Korea per year?
**A.** Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, foreign residents can remit up to **USD 50,000 per year** (across all banks and licensed fintech remittance apps combined) without submitting income verification documents. If you need to remit **more than USD 50,000 per year**—for example, when repatriating your accumulated salary or housing deposit (`전세보증금`) upon leaving Korea—you simply designate one primary Foreign Exchange Bank (`지정외국환은행`) and submit your National Tax Service **Certificate of Income Amount (`소득금액증명원`)** or employment/lease contracts to wire 100% of your verified legal funds abroad.
