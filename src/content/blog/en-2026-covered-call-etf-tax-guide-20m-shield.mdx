## 1. How Covered Call ETFs Actually Work in 2026

A covered call strategy involves <strong>purchasing an underlying asset (such as S&P 500 or Nasdaq 100 stocks) while simultaneously selling call options (the right to buy the asset at a strike price) to collect an upfront option premium</strong>.

### The 2026 Evolution: 'Target Daily' Option Selling
First-generation covered call ETFs (like QYLD) suffered from irreversible principal decay whenever the market rebounded. In 2024 to 2026, modern evolutions have addressed this:

* **JEPQ (US Direct)**: Invests 80% in underlying Nasdaq equities and only writes out-of-the-money (OTM) options via Equity-Linked Notes (ELNs) on 20% of the portfolio, capturing 60% to 70% of Nasdaq's upward momentum while delivering a 10.5% yield.
* **Target Daily Covered Calls (Korea-Listed)**: Utilizes 0DTE (zero days to expiration) daily options, selling tiny slices of options each morning. This protects 90%+ of the equity upside while generating consistent 7% to 12% annual target distribution yields.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Does investing in US-listed JEPQ bypass the ₩20M Korean tax threshold?
**No, absolutely not.** While US dividends are subjected to a 15% US withholding tax at source, the gross dividend amount is **fully counted towards your ₩20M annual financial income limit as a Korean resident**. If your aggregate US dividends and Korean bank interest exceed ₩20M, you must file a May Comprehensive Income Tax return in Korea.

### Q2. Can retirees with zero earned income lose health insurance dependent status?
**Yes, and this is the most common pitfall.** Even if you have zero salary or business income, the moment your annual dividend and interest earnings exceed **₩20,000,001**, the National Health Insurance Service automatically expels you from your spouse's or child's coverage. You will be billed monthly based on your home and vehicle value.

### Q3. Are option premiums in domestic covered call ETFs tax-free?
Historically, certain domestic exchange-traded derivatives enjoyed capital gains exemptions, leading some funds to market non-taxable distributions. However, under refined tax guidelines, **distributions from covered call ETFs are generally treated as dividend income subject to 15.4% withholding**. Relying on uncertain tax loopholes is risky; using an ISA or Pension account provides **100% legal, codified tax immunity**.
