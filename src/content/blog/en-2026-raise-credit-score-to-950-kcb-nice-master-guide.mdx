## 2. 3 High-Impact Tactics to Push Past 950 Points

### ① Maximize Your Credit Limit & Pre-Pay Large Purchases (선결제)
If your credit card limit is 3 million KRW and you spend 2.4 million KRW per month, your utilization ratio is **80%**—which NICE penalizes as liquidity stress.
* **Fix:** Raise your credit card limit to 10 million KRW in your card app (dropping utilization to 24%), or use the **"Immediate Pre-Payment (즉시결제/선결제)"** button 3 days before your statement closing date so the reported balance stays low.

### ② Combine Credit Card + Check Card (Debit) Usage
Both KCB and NICE award bonus points when a consumer uses a **Check Card (체크카드) for at least 300,000 KRW per month for 6 consecutive months** alongside responsible credit card usage, because check cards demonstrate cash-backed spending without debt risk.

### ③ Prioritize Paying Off High-Interest & Multiple Small Loans First
Holding 3 or more separate loans triggers a "multi-debtor" penalty even if the total amount is small. Consolidating or paying off the highest-interest or most recently originated loan first restores your credit tier fastest.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Does checking my credit score or running loan rate comparisons hurt my score?**  
A. **No—100% false.** Since October 2011, Korean law prohibits credit bureaus from penalizing consumers for credit inquiries. Checking your score daily or running multi-bank rate comparisons on Toss/Finda has zero impact on your KCB or NICE rating.

**Q2. How can foreign residents (expats) build a 900+ credit score in Korea?**  
A. Foreigners holding an ARC (Alien Registration Card) start with a thin-file score (~650–720). The fastest way for expats to cross 900 is: (1) use a Korean check card for 300k+ KRW/mo for 6 months, (2) obtain a deposit-collateralized credit card (질권설정 신용카드) to build on-time credit history, and (3) submit NHIS & NTS income certificates via Toss/NICE.

**Q3. Why did my score drop when I paid off and closed my oldest credit card?**  
A. Closing your oldest credit card shortens your "Average Length of Credit History" (13%–16% of your total score) and reduces your total available credit limit (increasing your utilization ratio). Always keep your oldest no-fee or low-fee card open.
