## 1. Why November—Not January—Determines Your "13th Month Salary"

Every salaried employee in South Korea completes their **Year-End Tax Settlement (연말정산)** in January, with the resulting tax refund or additional tax clawback settled on their February payslip. However, when the official Simplified Tax Service opens on January 15, **the fiscal year (January 1 – December 31) is already closed**—meaning you can no longer change how you paid for goods or make retroactive contributions to tax-sheltered accounts.

To give taxpayers an actionable window before year-end, the National Tax Service (NTS) opens the **Year-End Tax Settlement Preview Service (연말정산 미리보기)** on HomeTax every late October. By importing your **actual January–September card and cash receipt transactions** and projecting your full-year salary, the simulator calculates exactly how much more you need to spend on debit cards or deposit into pension accounts before December 31 to zero out your tax liability.

> **`Final Payable Tax = ( Earned Income - Personal, Card \& Housing Income Deductions ) × Tax Rate (6%–45%) - Tax Credits`**

## 3. Dual-Income Couple Allocation Rules: Medical vs. Card Spending

In Step 03 of the HomeTax Preview simulator, married dual-income couples can test different family deduction allocations to find the lowest combined household tax bill:
- **Allocate Dependent Personal Deductions (인적공제) to the Higher Earner:** Because personal deductions (₩1,500,000 per eligible child or senior parent) reduce taxable income at your **highest marginal tax bracket**, assigning dependents to the spouse in the 24% or 35% bracket saves significantly more tax than assigning them to a spouse in the 6% or 15% bracket.
- **Concentrate Medical Expenses (의료비) on the Lower Earner:** Medical expenses only qualify for the 15% tax credit on amounts **exceeding 3% of the claimant's gross salary**. If Spouse A earns ₩90M (3% hurdle = ₩2.7M) and Spouse B earns ₩40M (3% hurdle = ₩1.2M), paying all family hospital, dental, pharmacy, and eyeglasses bills with Spouse B's card clears the 3% hurdle ₩1,500,000 earlier!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. What does a minus sign (-) next to my estimated tax amount on HomeTax mean?
On all National Tax Service Year-End Tax Settlement forms, look at the bottom line titled **차감징수세액 (Net Tax After Withholding)**:
- **Minus Sign (`-₩850,000`):** You overpaid taxes during the year and will **receive a ₩850,000 cash refund** with your February salary!
- **Plus Sign (`+₩420,000`):** Your withholdings were insufficient, and **₩420,000 will be deducted** from your February salary unless you add more tax credits before December 31.

### Q2. Can foreign residents claim the Monthly Rent Tax Credit (월세 세액공제) and Card Deductions?
**Yes!** Since the 2021 tax law revision, foreign workers who qualify as Tax Residents of South Korea, earn **₩80,000,000 or less** in annual gross salary, do not own a home in Korea, and have filed their Change of Place of Stay (체류지변경신고) matching their lease contract are **100% eligible for the 15%–17% Monthly Rent Tax Credit** (up to ₩10M annual rent)—provided they elect the standard progressive tax calculation rather than the 19% flat expat tax rate.

### Q3. If I switched jobs mid-year in 2026, how should I use the Preview Simulator?
Enter the **combined full-year gross salary** from both your previous employer and your current employer. Keep in mind that while **Credit/Check Card, Medical, Education, and Insurance deductions** only count expenses paid during months you were actively employed, **Pension Savings, IRP, and Donations** count 100% even if paid during an unemployed gap month between jobs!
