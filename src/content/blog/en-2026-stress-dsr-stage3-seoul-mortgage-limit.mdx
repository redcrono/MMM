Consequently, homebuyers and expat residents seeking mortgages in the capital region face an unprecedented **reduction in borrowing power—slashing loan limits by up to 28% (nearly 100M to 250M KRW)**.

This verified report breaks down official Financial Services Commission (FSC) data, provides **exact before-and-after loan limit simulations across annual salary brackets (50M, 80M, and 100M KRW)**, and outlines concrete financing workarounds.

## 1. What is Stage 3 Stress DSR? (Seoul vs. Regional Dual-Track Policy)

**Stress DSR** is a macroprudential framework that calculates your borrowing limit not on your nominal interest rate, but by adding a **hypothetical stress rate buffer** to test whether you can withstand future interest rate hikes.

> **Crucial Rule:** The stress interest rate (+3.0%) is purely an **underwriting test metric**, not the actual interest rate you pay monthly. Your monthly repayments are still billed at your contract rate (e.g., 4.0%), but your **maximum approved principal drops significantly**.

## 4. Key Takeaways for Expat & Global Homebuyers in Korea

1. **Pre-Qualify Before Bidding**: Never rely on outdated online loan calculators. Always assume a 25% reduction from baseline figures when budgeting Seoul real estate.
2. **Consolidate Secondary Debt First**: High-interest credit loans and card loans severely restrict your DSR ceiling. Pay off high-rate secondary debt before submitting primary mortgage applications.
3. **Check Expat Policy Mortgage Eligibility**: If you are a permanent resident (F-5) or married to a Korean national (F-6), check your eligibility for subsidized government mortgage programs that bypass commercial Stress DSR restrictions.
