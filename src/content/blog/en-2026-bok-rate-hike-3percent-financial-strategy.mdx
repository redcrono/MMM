In a decisive move to tame runaway household leverage and cool speculative real estate fever across Seoul, the <strong>Bank of Korea (BOK) Monetary Policy Board officially raised its base interest rate by 25 basis points from 2.75% to 3.00%</strong> on August 27, 2026. This marks the central bank's second consecutive back-to-back rate hike following July's increase, restoring the <strong>3.0% benchmark rate era</strong> for the first time in 18 months.

The monetary tightening comes directly in response to Korea's total household credit crossing the historic <strong>2,000 Trillion KRW threshold (2,019.8 Trillion KRW)</strong>, driven by panic-buying across metropolitan real estate markets.

The return to a 3.00% benchmark creates a seismic shift in personal asset allocation: <strong>commercial bank fixed-term deposits are poised to surpass 4.0% p.a.</strong>, while floating-rate mortgage borrowing costs threaten to breach <strong>6.5% p.a.</strong>, dramatically raising debt-servicing burdens for leveraged households.

This briefing analyzes the three macroeconomic drivers behind the BOK rate decision, asset class winners and losers, and a 3-step financial survival playbook for savers and borrowers.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Will the Bank of Korea hike interest rates again this year?
BOK Governor Rhee stated that future decisions will depend strictly on whether household debt expansion and property prices decelerate convincingly. Markets should not expect rate cuts before early 2027.

### Q2. Is a 1-year or 2-year deposit more advantageous in a 3% rate environment?
When benchmark rates reach cyclical peaks, locking in <strong>2-year to 3-year fixed deposits</strong> allows savers to preserve high yields even after central banks eventually begin easing cycles.

### Q3. How quickly will credit loan and overdraft rates rise?
Credit loans are benchmarked directly against 6-month and 1-year bank debentures. The 25 bps rate hike will filter into prime credit line rates within 1 to 2 weeks, pushing unsecured borrowing rates to <strong>5.5%–6.8% p.a.</strong>
