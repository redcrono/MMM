For retirees, spouses, and parents registered as **National Health Insurance (NHIS) dependents (건강보험 피부양자)** under an employed family member, losing dependent status is one of the most expensive hidden tax shocks in South Korea. The moment you are disqualified, you are converted into a **Local Subscriber (지역가입자)**—triggering monthly health insurance bills assessed not only on your income, but also on your home property tax base and net assets, often costing **250,000 to 450,000 KRW every single month (3M–5.4M KRW/year)**.

The single most dangerous trap in 2026 is the **"1 KRW Registered Business Income Rule."** Here is the exact statutory breakdown of NHIS dependent income/property thresholds and how to legally defend your dependent qualification.

## 2. How to Defend Against Business, Pension, and Financial Income Traps

1. **Closing Dormant Business Registrations & Deducting Necessary Expenses:**
   * NHIS evaluates **Net Business Income Amount (사업소득금액 = Gross Revenue - Necessary Expenses)**, NOT gross sales. If a registered business has 15M KRW in revenue and 15M KRW (or more) in deductible expenses, net business income is 0 KRW, preserving dependent status.
   * If you have an inactive Business Registration Certificate, file for formal closure (폐업신고) on HomeTax immediately so accidental side income is not classified under registered business rules.
2. **Public Pension vs. Private Pension Rules:**
   * **Public Pensions (National Pension, Civil Servant, Military, Teachers):** 100% of the gross annual payout is counted toward the 20M KRW cap! If you receive 1,670,000 KRW/month (20.04M KRW/year) in National Pension, you lose dependent status.
   * **Private Pensions (IRP, Pension Savings):** As long as private pension withdrawals are kept within **15 million KRW per year** and you opt for separate taxation (3.3%~5.5%), they are **excluded** from the NHIS 20M KRW dependent income calculation!
3. **Shifting Interest & Dividends into ISA Accounts:**
   * Interest and dividends earned inside a Korean **Intermediary ISA** are 100% excluded from NHIS income assessment regardless of size.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. If my combined interest and dividends are 19.5M KRW (under 20M), does NHIS count it as 0 KRW?**  
A. **Wait—be extremely careful!** Under NHIS rules, if your combined financial income (interest + dividends) is **10,000,000 KRW or less**, it is ignored (counted as 0 KRW). However, if your financial income is **between 10,000,001 KRW and 20,000,000 KRW**, the *entire* amount is added to your other income (such as National Pension or freelance income) to test the 20M KRW total cap!

**Q2. Do overseas stock capital gains (US stock profits) cause NHIS dependent disqualification?**  
A. **No.** Capital gains from selling overseas stocks (or domestic stocks) are classified as Capital Gains Income (양도소득), which is NOT included in the NHIS dependent income calculation. (However, exceeding 1M KRW in capital gains does disqualify you from the year-end tax 1.5M KRW personal income deduction.)

**Q3. Does housing rental income of 4 million KRW/year disqualify me?**  
A. Even if housing rental revenue is under 20M KRW (eligible for separate taxation), if you registered as a rental business and have positive taxable rental income after expenses (over 4M KRW gross for unregistered, or over 10M KRW gross for registered), you will be disqualified.
